# Urban Agglomeration Model

_Status: conceptual and product doctrine. Last updated: 2026-06-08._

SevenT4 must not confuse official city limits with the city people actually
live in.

Official limits are necessary for data. They tell us where a municipal budget,
ward map, service register, elected body, or legal duty applies. But official
limits are not the same as the lived city. The lived city is made through
commuting, migration, rent, land conversion, police jurisdiction, labour
markets, logistics, schools, hospitals, caste and class settlement patterns,
gendered mobility, developer capital, and state infrastructure.

The site therefore needs two truths on screen at the same time:

- This is the official boundary used for this dataset.
- This is the wider urban agglomeration or city-region in which the place
  actually operates.

## Boundary Types

SevenT4 should distinguish at least five boundary types.

| Boundary | What it means | Product use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Municipal boundary | The legal local-government body, such as a municipal corporation or municipality | Budgets, wards, councillors, municipal services, Part IXA questions |
| Statistical urban agglomeration | Census or statistical grouping of contiguous urban settlements and outgrowths | Population scale, settlement comparison, official demographic baseline |
| Metropolitan planning region | Statutory or planning region, such as NCR, MMR, AUDA/GUDA, or BMRDA/GBA | Regional transport, land, environment, infrastructure, and cross-border planning |
| Functional urban region | The lived labour, commute, housing, consumption, logistics, and service region | Explaining how residents experience the city beyond legal limits |
| Political accountability region | The actual mix of MPs, MLAs, councillors, state departments, Union agencies, parastatals, courts, authorities, and finance channels | Naming who has power, money, files, votes, and blame |

The atlas can rely on clean data inside official limits while clearly warning
users when the official limit is smaller than the real city.

## User-Facing Rule

Every city page should answer:

1. What is the official city limit used by this map?
2. What is the larger urban agglomeration or city-region?
3. Which services and budgets are inside the official city limit?
4. Which problems spill across the limit?
5. Which public bodies govern the spillover?
6. What does this boundary mismatch hide from residents?

This is not a footnote. It is part of the political education of the atlas.

## City-Region Examples

Ahmedabad should not be explained only through AMC limits. The lived and
planned region includes the Ahmedabad-Gandhinagar axis, GIFT/Gandhinagar
capital-region dynamics, Sanand, Kalol, Kadi-side industrial and peri-urban
expansion where relevant, and AUDA/GUDA/state planning power. The seed map can
use AMC wards, but the public explanation should teach that Ahmedabad's economy
and land market extend through a larger regional corridor.

Mumbai should be treated as the Mumbai Metropolitan Region, not only the
Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai. The MMR includes Greater Mumbai, Thane,
Navi Mumbai, Kalyan-Dombivli, Vasai-Virar, Mira-Bhayandar, Bhiwandi-Nizampur,
Ulhasnagar, Panvel, municipal councils, villages, MMRDA planning power,
transport authorities, port/rail/airport infrastructure, coastal risk, and
commuter rail geographies. Mumbai's lived city is the metropolitan region.

Bengaluru must be tracked through the recent restructuring of urban governance:
the Greater Bengaluru Governance Act, the Greater Bengaluru Authority, the
replacement or dissolution of the old BBMP frame, multiple city corporations,
ward committees, BDA/BWSSB/BMRCL/BMTC/BESCOM/parastatal overlaps, and the
Bengaluru Metropolitan Land Transport Authority. The transport story should be
read through the National Urban Transport Policy 2006 and the UMTA principle:
million-plus cities need coordinated transport governance because fragmented
transport agencies produce fragmented urban life.

Delhi NCR is the clearest example of why this model is necessary. NCR is not
just Delhi enlarged on a map. It is a national-capital region built through
Union power, Delhi's elected but limited government, municipal bodies, regional
planning, state governments, development authorities, land markets, migration,
and a huge metropolitan economy.

## NCR In Law

NCR has two different legal problems layered together.

First, Delhi itself is a Union Territory with an elected legislature. Article
239AA gives Delhi a legislative assembly and council of ministers, but Delhi is
not a normal State. Public order, police, and land sit outside the normal
legislative competence of Delhi's elected assembly. The Lieutenant Governor,
Union government, Delhi NCT government, DDA, Delhi Police, MCD, NDMC, Delhi
Cantonment Board, and courts therefore all matter.

Second, NCR is an inter-state regional planning formation. The National Capital
Region Planning Board Act, 1985 creates a statutory planning frame for NCT
Delhi and participating States. NCRPB prepares regional plans and functional
plans, while participating States and the Union Territory prepare sub-regional
plans. That means Haryana and Uttar Pradesh are not suburbs of Delhi in law.
Gurugram is in Haryana. Noida and Greater Noida are in Uttar Pradesh. Their
planning, land, police, municipal, industrial-development, and fiscal chains
run through their own state systems.

SevenT4 should therefore treat NCR as a special city-region with sub-atlases:

- Delhi NCT
- MCD Delhi
- NDMC / Lutyens' Delhi
- Delhi Cantonment
- Gurugram / Haryana NCR
- Noida and Greater Noida / UP NCR
- Ghaziabad / UP NCR
- Faridabad and other Haryana NCR nodes
- NCR regional-planning layer

## NCR In Finance

There is no single NCR treasury.

Money moves through many channels:

- Union schemes, central ministries, DDA, central land, rail, metro, police,
  national highways, and capital-city infrastructure.
- Delhi NCT budget and departments.
- MCD, NDMC, and Delhi Cantonment budgets.
- Haryana and Uttar Pradesh state budgets.
- Gurugram, Faridabad, Noida, Greater Noida, Ghaziabad, and other local or
  authority budgets.
- Development-authority land monetization, leases, conversion charges,
  external development charges, impact fees, betterment charges, and developer
  obligations.
- Parastatal and utility finance: water, power, transport, sewerage, roads,
  waste, industrial areas, and housing.
- NCRPB loans, project finance, regional infrastructure funding, and financial
  intermediation.
- Private capital, real estate debt, household rent, informal payments, and
  employer-provided or market-provided services.

This is why a service failure cannot be mapped only to a councillor. A bus, a
drain, a road, a police response, a metro station, a land-use change, a sewer,
or a resettlement colony may sit in different fiscal circuits even when users
experience them as one city problem.

## NCR In Politics

NCR politics is multi-layered and conflictual.

Delhi has MPs, MLAs, councillors, an elected government, and an elected
municipal corporation, but it also has a Lieutenant Governor, Union-controlled
land and police powers, central institutions, courts, and national-party
capital politics. Haryana and Uttar Pradesh NCR towns have their own MPs, MLAs,
state governments, local bodies, development authorities, industrial
authorities, police chains, and district administrations.

The democratic problem is that residents live across one metropolitan economy
but vote into different accountability chains. A migrant worker may work in
Gurugram, live in Delhi, travel through Noida, depend on a contractor in one
state and a ration card in another, and face policing by an institution that is
not accountable to the elected body they imagine governs the city.

The atlas must show this split without pretending it can solve it.

## NCR In Culture, Economy, And Society

NCR is a national-capital society, not merely a municipal area.

It concentrates Parliament, ministries, courts, media, universities,
diplomacy, political parties, think tanks, NGOs, corporate offices, developers,
real estate, construction, service work, IT and BPO corridors, logistics,
industrial belts, coaching economies, domestic work, street vending,
hospitality, and large informal labour markets.

It is also a migration machine. People arrive from Uttar Pradesh, Bihar,
Haryana, Rajasthan, Punjab, Uttarakhand, the Northeast, Bengal, and across
India for government work, construction, domestic work, informal services,
education, care work, logistics, corporate employment, small trade, and
political proximity. The region is held together by migrants who often receive
the least secure housing, weakest services, and most fragile political
recognition.

The 2012 Delhi gang rape and the protests around it must be treated as part of
this urban-governance story, not as a sensational reference. It made visible a
gendered city-region problem: transport, policing, street lighting, work hours,
private buses, informal employment, public fear, media power, middle-class
mobilization, and women's right to move through the city. The safety question
cannot be placed only inside one municipal boundary because mobility, policing,
labour, housing, and infrastructure cross boundaries.

NCR also contains sharp caste, class, religious, regional, and citizenship
fault lines: unauthorized colonies, urban villages, resettlement colonies,
Jhuggi-Jhopri clusters, gated colonies, builder floors, farmhouses, labour
chowks, industrial settlements, Dalit bastis, Muslim neighbourhoods, student
districts, elite state zones, and peri-urban villages converted into land
markets.

The atlas should make those differences visible as urban structure, not as
background sociology.

## Population And Scale

Population numbers depend on the boundary.

Official Census 2011 data gives Delhi Urban Agglomeration as about 16.35
million people. UN World Urbanization Prospects 2025 estimates New Delhi at
about 30.2 million people under its urban-agglomeration method. The same UN
2025 table ranks Jakarta first at about 41.9 million, Dhaka second at about
36.6 million, Tokyo third at about 33.4 million, and New Delhi fourth at about
30.2 million. NCR as a statutory planning region is larger again and includes
districts from NCT Delhi, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, and Rajasthan.

SevenT4 should never show a population number without naming the boundary and
source:

- Municipal corporation population.
- Delhi NCT population.
- Census urban agglomeration population.
- UN-style urban agglomeration estimate.
- NCR statutory region population.
- Custom functional-region estimate.

The right user message is:

"This dataset is official for this boundary. The lived city is larger."

## Ambedkarite Test

Ambedkar's warning applies directly. Administrative form is not democratic
power by itself. A city-region can have many authorities, plans, schemes,
budgets, and elected offices while still denying people real democratic
control.

For every urban agglomeration, SevenT4 should ask:

- Where is the public?
- Where is the elected body?
- Where is the money?
- Where is the file?
- Where is the police power?
- Where is the land power?
- Where are caste and class hidden in the map?
- Where does the migrant worker appear?
- Where does the woman moving through the city appear?
- Where does the informal settlement appear?
- Where does the authority evade answerability?

## Product Implications

SevenT4 should add these concepts to the data and interface:

- `official_data_boundary`: the boundary used for a specific dataset.
- `governance_boundary`: the legal or administrative body responsible.
- `agglomeration_boundary`: the wider urban formation being explained.
- `functional_region_note`: plain-language explanation of the lived city.
- `spillover_issue`: a problem that crosses the official boundary.
- `authority_chain`: the institutions that control law, finance, land, police,
  infrastructure, and service delivery.
- `source_boundary_warning`: a visible warning when a number applies only to a
  limited boundary.
- `street_claim`: the public demand that can be made despite the boundary
  mismatch.

The site should let users move between:

- official map
- agglomeration map
- authority map
- money map
- social geography map
- public-demand view
