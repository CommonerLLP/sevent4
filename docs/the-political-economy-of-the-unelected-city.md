# The political economy of the unelected city: four axes of one machine

This atlas asks four questions of every Indian city. Apart, they look like separate grievances — bad
governance, crony deals, high bills, dying workers. Together they describe **one machine**, and the same
people work every lever. The four questions are: **who decides, who profits, who pays, and who labours.**

## Axis 1 — Who decides? *The state, not the city.*

Across all six cities, the `decided_by` tally for every major service is **≈0 city.** Chennai: 0 city, 7
state, 3 centre. Hyderabad: 0 city, 11 state. The elected corporation decided essentially *none* of the
arrangements it lives under. This is not neglect; it is **statute**. Water sits in its own Act creating an
unelected board — BWSSB (1964), CMWSSB (1978), HMWSSB (1989) — and land in another (the development
authority). The high-value functions were *legislated out* of the elected body. The colonial proof is exact:
the **Calcutta Improvement Trust (1911) — Anwesha Ghosh's case — was repealed and merged into KMDA in 2017**,
the depoliticised land machine completing its century arc by Act of the legislature. And the constitutional
root is one clause: **Article 243W says the State "may, by law, endow"** the city with functions —
*discretionary.* The state decides what the city is even allowed to decide. (`fragmentation/`, `laws/INDEX.md`.)

## Axis 2 — Who profits? *Land — and the people who hold it.*

The city's value is its land, and the machine routes that value to private hands two ways. First, by
**under-taxing it**: India raises only **~0.15% of GDP from property tax, against ~1.7% in advanced economies**
— a tenth of potential. As urbanisation lifts land values, the tax *lags*, and (the World Bank's words) the
undervalued roll *"effectively creates an implicit subsidy for property owners."* Second, by **capturing the
uplift** through the unelected development authority — Ghosh's *recoupment*, reborn as a fee schedule:
Mumbai's FSI-premium machine, Kolkata's KMDA (which inherited the CIT), the documented cases — **Dharavi to
Adani**, the **CAG's ₹788 cr** of losses in New Town/Rajarhat allotments, Chennai's value handed to
developers by **FSI *exemption*.** The owner is both the least-taxed and the best-resourced to fight
revaluation (Mumbai's CVS litigation, Bengaluru's stay orders). (`rent_capture/`, `property_tax_*`.)

## Axis 3 — Who pays? *The resident — regressively, on autopilot.*

The gap left by the un-taxed landowner is filled from the household. The progressive tax is *frozen* by
political design (blocked revaluations, electoral exemptions, even government departments defaulting — ₹4,400
cr owed in Hyderabad) **while regressive user fees rise.** Chennai bills a **flat ₹105/month per dwelling**
for water; Bengaluru a **garbage cess by house-size**; both put hikes on **automatic escalators** (Mumbai 8%,
Bengaluru 3%) insulated from any vote — and Bengaluru's betterment levy is pledged to **repay a ₹3,000 cr
loan**, the resident as collateral. In three of six cities the water bill comes from a **board you never
elected.** The biggest property levy, stamp duty (~5% to **~11% in Tamil Nadu**), bypasses the city entirely
for the state. This is Cooper's counterrevolution in a utility bill — **austerity for the resident,
extravagance for the asset-holder** — and its summary is one sentence: *the uncollected property tax is the
quiet engine of the rent-capture machine — a public subsidy to the speculator, paid for by the user fee.*
(`user_fees_*`, `property_tax_analyses.*`.)

## Axis 4 — Who labours? *Caste-subordinated workers, extracted twice.*

The city is built and cleaned by a reserve army that is, in India, **caste-sorted** (Harvey's circuit needs
Ambedkar's sorting). The surplus is taken at the workplace and again in reproduction (tanker water at **40×**
the municipal rate; rent to the slumlord; debt to the moneylender). The proof is a single accounting line:
the **BOCW construction-welfare cess — ₹87,479 cr collected from the workers' own labour, of which ₹38,210
cr (~44%) sits *unspent*** while ~38 construction workers die a day — extraction with the return-valve shut.
And sanitation is the limit case: the corporation's one undeniable in-house function, the "broom," is wielded
by **contract Dalit labour** (NAMASTE: ~97% of sewer-deaths are SC), dying on the sub-contracted layer the
robots never reach — under a 2025 **Supreme Court order** naming five of these six cities. (`labour_extraction.*`,
`contractualization_map.json`, `manual_scavenging_map.json`, `the-double-extraction.md`.)

## The machine

Put the four together and the circuit closes:

> **Under-tax the land** (subsidy up to the owner) · **over-charge the use** (squeeze down on the resident) ·
> **capture the uplift** (rent up to the developer) · **extract the labour** (surplus up from the worker) ·
> and **decide it all above the elected body** (democracy out).

Four thinkers name the four moves. **Ghosh** shows the machine is *colonial in origin* — the municipality was
a corporation built to recoup, not to serve, and the improvement trust → development authority lineage is
literal. **Cooper** gives its *fiscal form* — austerity for the wage and the public service, extravagance for
the asset; the progressive tax suppressed, the regressive fee on autopilot, the household as collateral.
**Harvey** gives the *circuit* — the secondary circuit of capital, accumulation by dispossession, the right to
the city denied to those who build it. **Ambedkar** gives the *sorting* — the division of labour as a division
of *labourers*, graded inequality as the mechanism that assigns the city's filth and danger by birth.

## Against the grain

The 74th Amendment (1992) promised that your city would be run by **people you elect**, with real power over
your **water, roads, health, and money.** Lay that promise against the four axes and it is inverted at every
one: the *money* is decided by the state and the boards; the *land* is a subsidy to those who own it; the
*water* is billed by a body you cannot vote out; and the *labour* that holds it all up is extracted and left
to die. The "unelected city" is not a malfunction of that promise. It is the older machine the promise was
laid over — and never displaced.
