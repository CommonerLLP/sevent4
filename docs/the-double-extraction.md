# The double extraction: rent from the land, surplus from the labour — a Harvey–Ambedkar reading

The atlas's other dossiers follow the *land* — who captures the value when a development authority
assembles a township or a developer wins Dharavi. This one follows the *labour*, because the same city
that channels rent upward also pumps surplus out of the migrant who pours its concrete and the Dalit who
clears its sewers. They are not two stories. In David Harvey's terms they are one circuit — the
**urbanization of capital**. Marx supplies the engine, Harvey the spatial form, and — the part Anglo-Marxism
keeps missing — **Ambedkar supplies the sorting mechanism**: in the Indian city the reserve army of labour
is not merely a class, it is a *caste order*.

## Harvey's three moves, in an Indian register

1. **The secondary circuit / the spatial fix.** When capital over-accumulates in production it "switches"
   into the **built environment** — real estate and infrastructure become a sink for surplus with nowhere
   else to go. India's land-and-construction boom *is* that switch: a wall of capital (domestic black money,
   NRI remittance, PE/REIT funds, developer debt) poured into concrete because concrete absorbs it. The
   development authority, the SPV, the metro-over-station mall are the *plumbing* of that secondary circuit.

2. **Accumulation by dispossession.** The circuit needs land, and it takes it — slum demolition and
   "redevelopment," peri-urban land pooling, SEZ and sports/Olympic-corridor acquisition, and the enclosure
   of commons (lakes, riverbanks, grazing land, water bodies fenced and financialised). Assets that were the
   poor's — a foothold, a commons, a tenancy — are transferred to capital. Harvey's word is exact: not the
   gentle expansion of a market but *dispossession*, and in India it falls overwhelmingly on Dalit, Adivasi,
   Muslim and OBC bodies.

3. **The right to the city, denied.** Those who *build* the city — and clean it, and feed it — are
   dispossessed *of* it: priced out, evicted, denied urban citizenship. The migrant who raised the tower
   cannot live in its shadow; the safai karmachari who keeps the city alive dies in its drains.

## The chain of extraction — who leeches, and how

Read upward from the worker; each link takes its cut.

- **The contractor — the pivot.** He sits *on* the labour and pockets the gap between the contract value and
  the suppressed wage. **Road contracts are the purest case** — recurring (build, dig up, rebuild), opaque,
  and quality-indifferent, because bad work *guarantees the next tender*. The "pothole economy" is not a
  failure of road-building; it is road-building *as a rent*. Sub-contracting chains (contractor → petty
  contractor → jamadar) dilute the wage and the liability at every step, so that when a worker dies in a
  trench or a sewer, no one up the chain is liable.

- **The broker state.** Above the contractor sits the politician–bureaucrat who *allocates* — the contract,
  the land-use change, the FSI, the licence — taking a cut at each node (kickback, black money, electoral
  funding). This is the real business of the **unelected city**: not service delivery but *allocation*. The
  councillor you elect is handed the result; the commissioner, the development-authority chair and the
  minister decide it.

- **The landlord, the water-tanker, the moneylender.** The most intimate extraction happens in the sphere of
  **reproduction** — accumulation by dispossession in daily life. The worker's wage is clawed back through
  **rent** to a slumlord (Ghosh's bustee, 1900, still standing), through **water bought by the pot** from a
  tanker in the settlement the pipe never reached, through **user fees** for what were meant to be rights
  (Cooper's regressive collateral), and through **debt** to the moneylender who financed the migration. The
  worker is exploited *twice*: once at the workplace, once at home.

- **The developer / corporation / foreign capital — the top.** The value the labour produced — the
  land-uplift, the finished asset — is captured as **rent and capital gain** (the rent-capture dossiers).
  This is Cooper's *extravagance*: the asset-holder's gain, publicly underwritten.

## The workers — and why caste is not a footnote

The extracted are concrete: **construction and migrant labour** (a circular reserve army from Bihar, UP,
Odisha, Jharkhand, Bengal, Chhattisgarh — informal, contract-less, debt-bonded, uninsured; the **BOCW cess**
collected in their name largely *unspent*); **sanitation/conservancy** workers (contract Dalit labour, dying
in the drains); **domestic workers, street vendors, head-loaders**; and now the **gig/platform** worker,
whose contractor is an app.

Pure class analysis stops here and misses the design. **Ambedkar** finishes the sentence: the division of
labour in the Indian city is a **division of labourers** — the reserve army is *caste-sorted*. The
contractor, the corporator and the board-member are disproportionately the dominant-caste merchant-
professional bloc (the "Brahmin–Baniya"); the labour is Dalit, Adivasi, OBC, Muslim. **Graded inequality**
is not a residue the city is slowly shedding; it is the *mechanism* by which the city assigns its filth and
its danger to specific bodies and calls it natural. Sanitation is the limit case — work allotted by birth,
not contract — but the same logic runs through every rung: who pours concrete, who dies in trenches, who is
evicted first.

## The synthesis

The city extracts **twice**: it channels the **rent** of land upward to capital and the dominant-caste broker
class, and it pumps the **surplus** of labour out of caste-subordinated migrant and informal workers — with
the broker state allocating both flows. **Harvey** gives the circuit; **Ambedkar** gives the sorting;
**Ghosh** shows it is colonial in origin (the municipality as a rent-and-bustee-labour machine from 1900,
the contracted scavenger, the slumlord); **Cooper** gives the fiscal signature (austerity for the worker's
wage, extravagance for the asset-holder's gain). The "unelected city" is the political form of all of it: a
governance machine the working classes built, clean, and cannot vote.
