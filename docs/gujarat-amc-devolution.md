# Gujarat → Ahmedabad Municipal Corporation (AMC): Actual Devolution Under the 74th Amendment

*Neutral, sourced reference for the SevenT4 atlas. Scope: how much of the 74th Constitutional Amendment Act (CAA, 1992) framework Gujarat has actually devolved to AMC across the "3 Fs" — Functions, Funds, Functionaries — plus the empowered-mayor question and Gujarat's standing in Janaagraha's ASICS benchmark.*

AMC operates under the **Gujarat Provincial Municipal Corporations Act, 1949** (the GPMC Act, originally the Bombay Provincial Municipal Corporations Act), not a post-74th-CAA rewrite. The Act predates and structurally constrains the 74th Amendment's intent: the executive sits with a state-appointed Municipal Commissioner, not the elected wing.

---

## 1. Functions — partial devolution; the high-value functions stay in-house

Janaagraha's ASICS 2017 city assessment states plainly: **"the municipal body in Ahmedabad handles only 10 functions as opposed to the 18 listed in the 74th Constitution Amendment Act."** Ahmedabad scores relatively *well* on devolution by Indian standards (~57% on the SevenT4 scorecard) precisely because the functions AMC keeps in-house are the core, capital-heavy civic services rather than the residual ones.

**Retained by AMC (in-house departments):**
- **Water supply** — AMC department
- **Sewerage / sanitation** — AMC department
- **Solid waste management** — AMC department
- **Roads, streetlights, drainage** — AMC department
- **Property tax assessment & collection** — AMC's Assessment & Tax Collection Department

**Pulled out into parastatals / SPVs / private hands (the fragmentation):**
| Function | Body | Control |
|---|---|---|
| Land / town planning / development control outside & at the urban fringe | **AUDA** (Ahmedabad Urban Development Authority), constituted 1978 under the Gujarat Town Planning & Urban Development Act, 1976 | **State** — TP schemes, land pooling, development plans; jurisdiction ~1,866 sq km |
| Metro rail | **GMRC** (Gujarat Metro Rail Corporation, formerly MEGA) | **State SPV** — 50:50 GoI–GoG joint venture |
| Electricity distribution | **Torrent Power Ltd** | **Private** licensee (regulated by GERC) |
| City bus | **AMTS** (Ahmedabad Municipal Transport Service) | **AMC** transport undertaking |
| BRT / Janmarg | **AJL** (Ahmedabad Janmarg Ltd) | **AMC SPV** — 100% AMC subsidiary, registered under Companies Act |

So "urban transport" and "urban planning / land use" — two of the 12th-Schedule subjects — are substantially outside the elected corporation: planning sits with state-controlled AUDA, metro with a state SPV (GMRC), and electricity is fully privatised (Torrent). Bus (AMTS) and BRT (AJL) remain under AMC, though run as ring-fenced undertakings/SPVs.

## 2. Funds — relatively high own-revenue, but state-dependent on the margin

AMC has genuine fiscal muscle compared with most Indian ULBs. Per India Ratings (FY25 provisional): **own revenue ≈ 50.5% of total revenue income** (FY24: ~58%), split as tax revenue ~23.5% and non-tax revenue ~27%. The balance comes from **state government grants and compensation grants in lieu of octroi** (octroi was abolished, leaving AMC dependent on state compensation transfers). Property tax is the anchor own-source; the state has delegated assessment and collection to AMC.

**State Finance Commission (SFC) record — weak:** Gujarat's SFC track record is among the laggards. As of the 15th Finance Commission period, Gujarat had constituted only **three SFCs** (against the six due), with reports running only to ~2011, and was repeatedly flagged for delays before belatedly coming into compliance. Irregular SFCs mean the vertical transfer formula to ULBs is not refreshed on the constitutional 5-year cycle, weakening predictable fiscal devolution.

## 3. Functionaries — state cadre, not a municipal cadre

AMC is **not** staffed by an autonomous municipal cadre at the top. The **Municipal Commissioner is a state-appointed IAS officer**, deputed by the Gujarat General Administration Department, and under the GPMC Act the Commissioner — not the Mayor — is the **chief executive authority** of the corporation (budget, staffing, day-to-day administration). ASICS's national finding that "mayors and city councils have limited authority in appointing and promoting staff" applies directly: senior appointments and promotions are controlled via the state, and ~35% of posts in India's municipal corporations sit vacant.

## 4. Empowered mayor? — No. Ceremonial by design.

The ASICS Ahmedabad fact sheet is explicit: **"The mayor of Ahmedabad is indirectly elected by its citizens and has a 2.5-year term."** The mayor is chosen by elected corporators (not directly by voters), serves half a council term, and holds a largely **ceremonial** role. Real executive power rests with the **IAS Municipal Commissioner** appointed by the state. This is the structural pattern ASICS flags for Indian megacities: *"Mayors in megacities are not directly elected and do not have a five-year tenure."* Verdict: **AMC has no empowered mayor — the executive is the unelected, state-deputed Commissioner.**

## 5. Gujarat / Ahmedabad standing on devolution (ASICS)

- **ASICS 2017 (latest with a published Ahmedabad city score):** Ahmedabad scored **4.4 / 10, ranked 7 of 23** cities (a 1.1-point rise from 2016; top city Pune 5.1). Pillar scores: Urban Planning & Design 3.5, Urban Capacities & Resources ~5.6, Empowered & Legitimate Political Representation ~5.0, Transparency/Accountability/Participation 3.5. The weak pillars are exactly the political-empowerment and transparency dimensions — consistent with the ceremonial-mayor / Commissioner-run structure above.
- Voter turnout in the last municipal election (46.2%) ran well below the state-election turnout (66.6%), a signal of the corporation's diminished political salience.
- **ASICS 2023** moved to evaluating the *quality of state urban laws* across all 35 states/UTs (no single city score); its national findings — megacity mayors neither directly elected nor 5-year-tenured, councils lacking staffing/finance powers, only Assam empowering ULBs to collect all key taxes — describe the Gujarat/GPMC configuration well.

---

### Bottom line (≈14 lines)
1. AMC runs under the pre-74th **GPMC Act, 1949**, which vests the executive in the state, not the council.
2. **Functions:** AMC handles only ~10 of the 18 12th-Schedule subjects (ASICS 2017) — but keeps the core ones in-house.
3. **In-house:** water, sewerage, solid waste, roads — hence Ahmedabad's relatively high ~57% devolution score for India.
4. **Carved out:** land/planning → **AUDA (state)**; metro → **GMRC (state SPV)**; electricity → **Torrent Power (private)**.
5. **Transport split:** bus **AMTS** (AMC) and BRT **AJL** (100% AMC SPV) stay with the city; metro does not.
6. **Funds:** own revenue ~50% of income (FY25) — strong by Indian standards, anchored on property tax.
7. The rest is **state grants + octroi-compensation transfers**, so AMC is autonomous on revenue but exposed at the margin.
8. **SFC record is weak:** Gujarat constituted only ~3 of 6 due State Finance Commissions, a recurring decentralisation gap.
9. **Functionaries:** no autonomous municipal cadre — the **Municipal Commissioner is a state-appointed IAS officer** and the legal chief executive.
10. Council/mayor have limited say over senior staffing and promotions (state-controlled).
11. **Empowered mayor? No.** Indirectly elected, **2.5-year term**, ceremonial.
12. Real power = the **unelected IAS Commissioner** — the defining accountability gap.
13. **ASICS 2017:** Ahmedabad **4.4/10, rank 7/23**; weakest on political empowerment and transparency.
14. **Verdict:** Ahmedabad is a *high-capacity, low-democratic-control* corporation — strong on funds/functions, hollow on the empowered-mayor and functionary dimensions.
