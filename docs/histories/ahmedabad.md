# Ahmedabad: a council older than the Republic, a power newer than you think

**Incorporated as a Municipal Corporation: 1950** · under the **Bombay Provincial Municipal Corporations Act, 1949** · devolution score today: **77%** (10 of 13 resident-facing services run by the elected corporation).

## The dates that matter

The Amdavad Municipal Corporation likes to call itself *"the first people's representative council in India."* The claim is about elected self-government, and the lineage behind it is unusually deep:

- **1831** — A flood damages the city's fort wall. The Collector convenes the citizens and forms the **Town Wall Fund Committee** (21 April 1831), levying a 1% cess on the sale of ghee and other commodities. Two lakh rupees are raised; the wall is repaired. This is the seed: a civic body that taxes the city to maintain the city.
- **1885** — **Sheth Ranchhodlal Chhotalal** is made the first President of the municipality (15 September 1885) — *nominated* by the British, not elected.
- **1915** — The "Republic" (representative) municipality comes into being (1 April 1915). **Rao Bahadur Bhaishankar Nanabhai** becomes the first *elected* President. This is the claim's real basis: an elected municipal head, years before India had a Republic of its own.
- **1925** — The Ahmedabad Borough Municipality.
- **1935** — The municipality's centenary.
- **1950** — The **Amdavad Municipal Corporation** is constituted under the Bombay Provincial Municipal Corporations Act, 1949. The office of *President* becomes *Mayor*. **Sheth Chinubhai Chimanlal** has the unique distinction of being the last President and the first Mayor. The central hall is named Gandhi Hall; the administrative building, Sardar Patel Bhavan.

A careful note, because the atlas does not flatten history: AMC's "first in India" is a claim about **elected** self-government. The oldest **corporation** in India is Madras (1688) — but that was a colonial body, not a people's council. Both things are true; they are not the same claim.

## Why Ahmedabad is a child of Bombay law

The decisive fact is the **1949 Act**. Ahmedabad's corporation is not a Gujarati invention — it is constituted under the **Bombay** Provincial Municipal Corporations Act, the same statute that governs Pune (incorporated weeks later, in February 1950) and, after Gujarat split from Bombay state in 1960, was simply inherited and renamed the **GPMC Act**. Surat and Vadodara run under it too.

That shared legal DNA is why Ahmedabad keeps so much in-house. Under the BPMC template, water supply, sewerage, roads, solid waste, the city bus (AMTS), fire and public health are all **corporation** functions — the elected body's own departments. That is the 77%.

## What was still taken

A high score is not a clean one. The capital-intensive, contract-rich layer has been pulled out of the elected corporation and into state-controlled vehicles:

- **Land and planning → AUDA** (Ahmedabad Urban Development Authority, 1978) — a state board, not the council.
- **The metro → GMRC**, a 50:50 Centre–State SPV, JICA-funded, entirely off the municipal books.
- **The BRT (Janmarg) → AJL**, a special-purpose vehicle.
- **Electricity → Torrent Power**, privatised distribution.

This is the same hollowing the rest of the atlas documents — it just arrived *later* in Ahmedabad, and on top of a genuinely strong council, rather than instead of one. The 74th Amendment was meant to deepen the 1915 promise of elected local government. In Ahmedabad it met a corporation that already ran the city — and watched the newest, costliest functions route around it anyway.
