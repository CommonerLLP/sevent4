# Surat & Vadodara: the Bombay-Presidency template, proved twice

Ahmedabad's lesson — that a city's devolution is set by the *state's* municipal law, not by the city's age or competence — is not an Ahmedabad quirk. It is the rule. Surat and Vadodara are the proof. Both run under the **same statute** as Ahmedabad: the **Bombay Provincial Municipal Corporations Act, 1949**, inherited by Gujarat as the GPMC Act. So both carry Ahmedabad's profile: a strong in-house corporation doing water, sewerage, roads and solid waste — with land and planning pulled out into a state development authority.

Neither city is in the atlas's data set yet. There is no computed devolution score for Surat or Vadodara — we will not invent a percentage. But the legal DNA is identical to Ahmedabad's, and the institutional shape that follows from it is visible without the arithmetic.

## Surat

- **Municipality: 1852.** Incorporated as the **Surat Municipal Corporation in 1966**, under the Bombay Provincial Municipal Corporations Act, 1949 — the same Act as Ahmedabad and Pune.
- **Development authority: SUDA** (Surat Urban Development Authority), **1978** — the land-and-planning layer lifted out of the elected corporation into a state board.

Surat is the inconvenient case for anyone who thinks the unelected city is just a story of municipal failure. SMC is widely cited as **India's best-run municipal corporation** — the in-house service-delivery turnaround after the 1994 plague is the textbook example of an elected body fixing its own city. Yet even here, the development function sits in SUDA, not the council. Under the GPMC template, water, sewerage, roads and solid waste are corporation functions (in-house); electricity is privatised to a DISCOM (a Torrent-type distributor — verify); land goes to SUDA. The strength of the corporation does not buy back the powers the *law* routed around it.

## Vadodara (Baroda)

- **Municipality: 1892.** Incorporated as the **Vadodara Municipal Corporation in 1950**, under the Bombay Provincial Municipal Corporations Act, 1949.
- **Development authority: VUDA** (Vadodara Urban Development Authority), **1978**.

Vadodara is the twist that makes the whole argument. Baroda was **not** a Bombay-Presidency city. It was a **princely state** — the Gaekwads' — outside British provincial administration entirely. By the third lineage in this atlas, Baroda belongs with the princely-state capitals, not with Ahmedabad, Pune and Surat.

And yet it ends up governed by exactly the same Bombay statute. In **1949 Baroda State merged into Bombay state**, and with the merger it inherited the BPMC/GPMC Act. The 1950 corporation was constituted under it. A princely capital, by absorption, was handed the Bombay-Presidency template — and with it the same high-devolution profile and the same VUDA-shaped hole.

## What it proves

Three different lineages — Presidency capitals, Bombay-Presidency cities under the 1949 Act, and princely-state capitals — and Baroda jumps tracks between two of them by an administrative merger. Devolution did not follow Baroda's princely history. It followed the *law Baroda was placed under*.

That is the atlas's thesis in one frame. Devolution is set by the **state legislature's municipal Act**, not by the individual city's antiquity, prestige, or performance. Gujarat's cities cluster high not because each chose well but because they share the GPMC Act. The mechanism is always the same one Ghosh named: the improvement trust becomes the development authority, and that authority holds the land *outside* the elected corporation. Surat shows it does not depend on a weak council. Baroda shows it does not depend on Presidency ancestry. Same Act, same shape, twice over.
