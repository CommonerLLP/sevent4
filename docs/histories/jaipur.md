# Jaipur: the corporation came late, the land authority came first

**Municipality: 1869** · **Incorporated as a Municipal Corporation: 1994** (verify) · under the **Rajasthan Municipalities Act** · split into **two corporations — Greater and Heritage — in 2019** · devolution score today: **54%** (7 of 13 resident-facing services run by the elected JMC).

## The dates that matter

Jaipur is old. Its municipality, on the records the atlas holds, dates to **1869** (verify) — older than most of the councils in this collection. But age is a trap here, because age and *power* don't move together. The thing a citizen actually wants — an elected corporation with real control over the city — arrived in Jaipur strikingly late.

- **1869** (verify) — A municipality is formed. This is administration, not self-government in the modern sense; the seat of a princely Kachhwaha state.
- **1982** (verify) — The **Jaipur Development Authority (JDA)** is created. Note the order carefully: the land-and-planning authority predates the corporation it would later overshadow.
- **1994** (verify) — Jaipur is finally constituted as a **Municipal Corporation**, under the Rajasthan Municipalities Act. The *corporation* form — the thing the 74th Amendment was about — reached Jaipur only *after* the 1992 Amendment forced the issue.
- **2019** (verify) — The corporation is split into **two**: Jaipur Greater and Jaipur Heritage. One elected body becomes two.

## A princely capital, devolved late

The atlas tracks three lineages. The Presidency capitals (Calcutta, Madras, Bombay) carry corporations seeded under colonial rule. The Bombay-Presidency cities (Ahmedabad, Pune, Surat) share the BPMC template that keeps water, sewerage, buses and roads inside the elected body — and that is why Ahmedabad scores 77%.

Jaipur belongs to the **third group: the princely-state capitals** — Hyderabad, Bangalore, Cochin, Jaipur — capitals of states that were never Presidencies and never inherited that template. In Jaipur, the elected *corporation* is a creature of **1994**, while the **JDA dates to 1982**. The parastatal carve-out here is not a late betrayal of a strong council, as in Ahmedabad. The land authority was simply standing first, and the corporation was built — recent and thin — beside it.

This is the pattern Ghosh names: the colonial **improvement trust** becomes the **development authority**, and that authority holds the land *outside* the elected corporation. Devolution does not track age. A municipality of 1869 can still be among the least-devolved cities in the atlas.

## What was taken

The elected JMC keeps the visible, low-capital work: roads (local; the arterials are largely JDA), solid waste, street lighting, storm-water (with JDA), parks (with JDA; the major parks are JDA's), and public health. Fire is in-house (verify). That is the 54%.

Pulled out of the elected body:

- **Water → PHED** — a state department, not the council.
- **Sewerage → RUIDP / PHED** (verify) — state again.
- **City bus → JCTSL**, a special-purpose vehicle.
- **The metro → JMRC**, an SPV, off the municipal books.
- **Land and planning → JDA (1982)** — dominant; it holds much of the road and land a corporation elsewhere would own outright.

Electricity (**JVVNL**) and police sit in the state column as context.

So Jaipur is a princely capital whose corporation is recent and weak: JDA and PHED predate or outweigh it on the two things that decide a city — land and water. One of the least-devolved in the atlas, and split in two since 2019. The 74th Amendment said this would be different.
