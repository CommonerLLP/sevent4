# Pune: the same law as Ahmedabad, so the carve-out is the whole story

**Incorporated as a Municipal Corporation: 1950** · under the **Bombay Provincial Municipal Corporations Act, 1949** · devolution score today: **69%** (9 of 13 resident-facing services run by the elected PMC).

## The dates that matter

Pune's civic body is old, but its powers were not handed down intact. The timeline is short and the lesson is sharp:

- **1858** — A municipality is set up for the city. A body that taxes Pune to maintain Pune — the same seed civic form the rest of this atlas tracks.
- **1950** — The **Pune Municipal Corporation** is constituted (15 February 1950), under the Bombay Provincial Municipal Corporations Act, 1949. This is the decisive date, and it is barely weeks after Ahmedabad's own incorporation under the *identical* statute.
- **2015** — The **Pune Metropolitan Region Development Authority (PMRDA)** is created — a state board that lifts land and planning out of the elected corporation's hands.

The gap between 1950 and 2015 is the gap this atlas exists to measure: a strong elected corporation first, the quiet routing-around of it later.

## A child of Bombay law

Pune is the cleanest "tell" of the Bombay-Presidency family. It is not a Presidency capital like Madras (1688), Calcutta (1876) or Bombay (1888) — the oldest, strongest corporations. It belongs to the second lineage: the cities constituted under the **1949 Act** — Ahmedabad, Pune, Surat — which inherited that strong template wholesale.

That shared legal DNA matters because it controls what stays in-house. Under the BPMC template, water supply, sewerage, roads, solid waste, street lighting, storm-water drainage, parks, public health and fire are all **corporation** departments — the elected body's own. That is the 69%. Same statute as Ahmedabad, same instinct to keep the core services with the council you vote for.

So when something *isn't* in-house in Pune, it is not because the law was weak. It is because it was deliberately pulled out — and that makes Pune the case where you can see the carve-out with nothing else in the way.

## What was taken

The score is 69% because the entire mobility-and-land layer — the capital-intensive, contract-rich functions — has been routed out of the elected corporation:

- **City bus → PMPML**, a special-purpose vehicle jointly owned by PMC and the neighbouring Pimpri-Chinchwad corporation. The bus you ride is run by an SPV, not a department.
- **BRT → Rainbow BRTS**, another SPV.
- **Metro → Maha Metro and PMRDA**, an SPV, off the municipal books.
- **Urban planning and land → PMRDA**, the 2015 state board.

Electricity (MSEDCL) and police are state subjects — context, not scored against the corporation.

This is the pattern Anwesha Ghosh names: the colonial "improvement trust" reincarnated as the modern "development authority," holding the city's land and planning *outside* the body residents elect. Devolution does not track age — Pune kept the same strong 1949 template Ahmedabad did, and still watched its transport and its land route into SPVs and a state authority. The 74th Amendment was meant to deepen elected local government. In Pune it met a capable corporation and let the costliest functions go around it.
