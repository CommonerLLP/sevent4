# Chennai: the oldest corporation in India, and one of the most hollowed

**Incorporated as a Municipal Corporation: 1688** · by **Royal Charter, 29 September 1688** (later the Madras City Municipal Corporation Act, 1919) · devolution score today: **50%** (6 of 12 resident-facing services run by the elected corporation).

## The dates that matter

- **1688** — The **Corporation of Madras** is established by Royal Charter — **the oldest municipal corporation in India**, older than every other body in this atlas by roughly two centuries. Madras had an elected-by-charter civic corporation before most of the world's cities had anything of the kind.
- **1919** — The **Madras City Municipal Corporation Act** gives the body its modern statutory form.
- **1972** — The **CMDA** (Chennai Metropolitan Development Authority) is created — planning and land are lifted out of the corporation.
- **1978** — **Chennai Metrowater** (CMWSSB) is constituted as a separate statutory board — water and sewerage are lifted out too.
- **1996 / 2011** — The city is renamed Chennai (1996); the corporation becomes the Greater Chennai Corporation (2011).

## The central irony of the atlas

Chennai is a **presidency capital** — the founding case, in fact. If the strength of a corporation tracked its age, Chennai would be the most powerful local government in India. It is among the weakest in *functional* terms.

The 1688 corporation, three centuries on, no longer runs the things that most shape a resident's life. **Water and sewerage** went to Metrowater (1978). **Planning and land** went to CMDA (1972). **City buses** are run by the state (MTC). **The metro** is a state SPV (CMRL). What the Greater Chennai Corporation still runs is the labour-intensive, low-glamour half: **roads, solid waste (a vast conservancy operation), street lighting, storm-water drains, parks, public health.** It holds the broom; the state holds the pipes, the trains and the land.

This is why the atlas insists that **devolution does not track age.** The hollowing of Chennai happened in the 1970s — the CMDA-and-Metrowater decade — when Tamil Nadu, like much of southern India, built specialist state parastatals and pulled the highest-value civic functions out of the elected corporation in the name of technocratic efficiency. The oldest people's corporation in the country was reduced to a sanitation authority with a mayor.

## What was taken

- **Water + sewerage → Chennai Metrowater (CMWSSB)**, a state board, 1978.
- **Planning + land → CMDA**, a state board, 1972.
- **City bus → MTC**, a state transport undertaking.
- **Metro → CMRL**, a state SPV.

(Electricity and police were never municipal functions, so they are context, not part of the score.)

The 74th Amendment, in 1992, was supposed to reverse exactly this — to return funds, functions and functionaries to elected corporations. In Chennai it arrived two decades *after* the carve-out, and left it almost entirely intact. The country's first corporation is still waiting for the Amendment's promise to reach it.
