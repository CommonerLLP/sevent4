# Bhubaneswar: the planners arrived before the voters

**India's first major post-independence planned capital** (Otto Königsberger plan, **1948**) · municipal corporation **1994** (later under the **Orissa Municipal Corporation Act, 2003**) · devolution score today: **55%** (6 of 11 resident-facing services run by the elected BMC).

## The dates that matter

Every other lineage in this atlas inherits its municipal body from somewhere older than itself — a colonial Presidency town, a Bombay-Presidency borough, a princely durbar. Bhubaneswar inherits nothing. It was *drawn*. The capital of Odisha was a deliberate act of design, laid out on Otto Königsberger's plan in **1948**, the Republic's first major planned capital. And that origin produces an inversion you can read straight off the calendar.

- **1948** — Königsberger's plan for the new capital. The city is conceived as an administrative object — zoned, sectored, planned — before it is a community that governs itself.
- **1979** *(verify)* — A municipality is constituted. The city has lived as a planned settlement for three decades by now.
- **1983** — The **Bhubaneswar Development Authority (BDA)** is created. Hold this date.
- **1994** — Bhubaneswar is incorporated as a **Municipal Corporation** — the elected **BMC** — later governed by the Orissa Municipal Corporation Act, 2003.

Set 1983 against 1994 and the point lands: **the planning authority predates the elected council by eleven years.** The body that holds the city's land and drafts its future existed before the body its citizens vote for. The democratic promise was not eroded here over time — it was inverted at the start, by construction.

## A capital with no older self

The three familiar lineages don't fit. Bhubaneswar has no Presidency charter, no Bombay-Provincial-Act DNA, no royal municipality renamed after Independence. It is the rarer fourth thing: a *post-independence planned capital*, and its distinctive pathology follows from that.

The lesson Ghosh draws elsewhere holds in its purest form here: **devolution does not track age.** A council can be old and powerful (Ahmedabad, an elected President in 1915) or new and hollow. Bhubaneswar is newer than most — and yet its land sits *outside* the elected corporation, held by an authority older than the corporation itself. Being designed late did not make the city more democratic. It meant the planners simply got there first.

## What was taken

Of the eleven functions a resident relies on, the elected BMC runs six. In-house: **city roads** (major roads also BDA/State Works), **solid waste**, **street lighting**, **storm-water drainage**, **parks** (with BDA, *verify*), and **public health**. That is the 55%.

Pulled out of the elected body:

- **Water and sewerage → WATCO / PHEO / OWSSB** *(verify)* — state utilities, not the council.
- **City bus (Mo Bus) → CRUT**, a special-purpose vehicle.
- **Land and planning → BDA** (1983) and the state board — the authority that came *before* the corporation, and still outranks it on land.

There is no metro. Electricity (**TPCODL**, the Tata Power JV) and police sit outside the corporation as context, as in every city here.

The 74th Amendment was meant to make the elected corporation the city's government. In Bhubaneswar it met a corporation that was, by design, the *junior* institution — handed the city eleven years after the planners had already taken the land.
