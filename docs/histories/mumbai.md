# Mumbai: the strongest council in India, and what still got away

**Incorporated as a Municipal Corporation: 1888** · under the **Bombay Municipal Corporation Act, 1888** (still in force) · devolution score today: **83%** (10 of 12 resident-facing services run by the elected corporation) — the highest in the atlas.

## The dates that matter

- **1865** — A municipal commissioner is appointed for Bombay; the city begins building a professional civic administration.
- **1888** — The **Bombay Municipal Corporation Act** constitutes the corporation in the form it still holds. This is the single most consequential municipal statute in India: it gives the BMC (today MCGM) capacious, in-house powers that most other corporations never received or later lost.
- **1898** — After the 1896 plague, the **Bombay City Improvement Trust** is created — the prototype of the "improvement regime" the historian Anwesha Ghosh analyses: a technocratic, land-holding body set up beside the elected municipality to "improve" the city through property and finance.
- **1975** — The **MMRDA** is constituted as the metropolitan planning and infrastructure authority — the modern heir to that trust logic, holding regional land and the big projects outside the elected corporation.

## A presidency capital — and it shows

Bombay is one of the three **presidency capitals** (with Madras and Calcutta) that received the earliest and strongest corporations. But Bombay's 1888 Act is the outlier even among them. Under it, the BMC runs — *as its own departments* — water supply, sewerage, the storm-water system (BRIMSTOWAD), roads, solid waste, street lighting, parks, the fire brigade, the **BEST** bus undertaking, and an unusually deep public-health estate: the BMC owns and runs major teaching hospitals (KEM, Sion, Nair). Very few Indian corporations run hospitals. Almost none run their own electricity supply, yet BEST does, for the island city.

That breadth is the 83%. When residents say "the BMC," they mean a body that genuinely controls most of the services they touch — the opposite of the hollowed corporation elsewhere in the atlas.

## What still got away

Even the strongest council does not hold everything, and what it lost is telling:

- **Land and big infrastructure → MMRDA** (1975) — a state board that plans the metropolitan region and builds its flagship projects.
- **The metro → MMRC / MMRDA** — a state SPV layer (Line 3 by MMRC), entirely outside BMC.

These are exactly the two functions the atlas watches get pulled out everywhere: **land and the prestige transit project.** In a corporation this strong, they are the *only* major carve-outs — which makes Mumbai the clearest proof of the pattern. The 74th Amendment promised every Indian city a corporation like this one. Bombay had it since 1888. The Amendment's job was to make the other cities resemble Bombay; instead, the newest and costliest functions in Bombay itself were routed around even *its* council.
