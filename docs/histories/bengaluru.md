# Bengaluru: a corporation that runs the broom but not the pipes, the trains, or the land

**Municipality: 1862** · **Municipal Corporation: 1949** (City of Bangalore Municipal Corporation Act) · **BBMP (Greater Bangalore): 2007** · devolution score today: **50%** (6 of 12 resident-facing services run by the elected corporation).

## The dates that matter

- **1862** — Bangalore gets a municipality. This is a princely-state lineage, not a Presidency one: the city is the capital of Mysore, governed under its own genealogy rather than the colonial corporation tradition of Madras (1688), Calcutta (1876) or Bombay (1888).
- **1945** — The **City Improvement Trust Board (CITB)** is set up. Hold this date; it is the hinge of the whole story.
- **1949** — The city is incorporated as a Municipal Corporation under the **City of Bangalore Municipal Corporation Act, 1949**.
- **1964** — Water and sewerage are hived off to the **Bangalore Water Supply & Sewerage Board (BWSSB)**, a state board. The pipes leave the council.
- **1976** — The CITB becomes the **Bangalore Development Authority (BDA)**. Land and planning are now a state body's, not the council's.
- **2007** — The **Bruhat Bengaluru Mahanagara Palike (BBMP)** is constituted for Greater Bangalore.
- **2024** — The **Greater Bengaluru Governance Act** splits BBMP itself.

## Three lineages, one carve-out

India's city corporations do not all descend from the same root. There are the Presidency capitals — Madras, Calcutta, Bombay — colonial bodies, old but never people's councils. There are the Bombay-Presidency cities incorporated under the **BPMC Act, 1949** — Ahmedabad, Pune, Surat. And there are the princely-state capitals — Hyderabad, Bangalore/Mysore, Jaipur, Cochin — with their own separate genealogies.

The point is that devolution does **not** track age. The hollowing-out is younger than any of these lineages. As Anwesha Ghosh frames it, the colonial *improvement trust* became the modern *development authority* — a body that holds the city's land and planning power **outside** the elected corporation. Bengaluru is the textbook case: CITB in 1945, BDA in 1976, an unbroken line of succession that kept land permanently beyond the council's reach. The parastatal carve-out is a post-1960s invention, bolted onto a corporation that predates it by a century.

## What was taken

BBMP keeps the visible, low-margin street layer — and it owns those failures squarely:

- **Roads, storm-water drains (rajakaluve/SWD), street lighting, public health** — in-house.
- **Solid waste** (BBMP/BSWML) — in-house. The notorious garbage crisis is the corporation's *own* function, not someone else's.
- **Parks** — BBMP's, though Lalbagh and Cubbon are state Horticulture.

The high-value, contract-rich functions were pulled out into state boards and SPVs:

- **Water + sewerage → BWSSB** (state board, 1964).
- **City bus → BMTC** (state). **Metro → BMRCL / Namma Metro** (SPV).
- **Land + planning → BDA** (state board). Electricity (BESCOM) and police sit in state hands as context.

So the arithmetic is brutal. BBMP runs the broom — roads, garbage, drains — but not the pipes, the trains, or the land. The "mess" Bengaluru is famous for — the garbage, the flooding, **and no elected council since 2020** — sits with a corporation stripped of its highest-value functions and, lately, of its very shape. The 74th Amendment promised an elected city government with real power. Here it found a council old enough to predate the Republic, and watched its water, its transit, and its ground route around it anyway.
