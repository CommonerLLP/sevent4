# Kanpur: the factory city that lost its water and its land to the state

**Municipality (Cawnpore): 1861** · **Incorporated as a Municipal Corporation: 1959**, under the **Uttar Pradesh Nagar Mahapalika Adhiniyam, 1959** · devolution score today: **50%** (6 of 12 resident-facing services run by the elected Kanpur Nagar Nigam).

## The dates that matter

Kanpur is one of India's oldest industrial municipalities, and its lineage is neither the one the textbooks reach for nor the one this atlas usually documents. It is not a Presidency capital like Madras or Calcutta. It is not a Bombay-Presidency city built under the BPMC template, like Ahmedabad or Pune. And it is not a princely-state capital. It is a **United Provinces** city — a provincial, industrial town — and that origin shaped everything about how power was carved out of it.

- **1861** — A municipality is constituted at Cawnpore. This is an early date by any measure: a civic body taxing and servicing a mill town decades before most Indian cities had one.
- **1919** — The **Kanpur Improvement Trust** is established. This is the detail that matters most. The "improvement trust" is the colonial instrument Asher Ghosh studies: a state-appointed body, sitting *beside* the municipality, given control over land and city-shaping — never accountable to the ratepayer. Kanpur, an industrial city, got one of exactly this kind. The pattern is not unique to the grand capitals; it followed the factories.
- **1959** — Cawnpore is reconstituted as a **Municipal Corporation** under the **Uttar Pradesh Nagar Mahapalika Adhiniyam, 1959** — its own provincial statute, not a Bombay one. Nearly a century after the municipality, the city finally gets a corporation.
- **1973–74** — The **UP Urban Planning & Development Act, 1973** creates the **Kanpur Development Authority (KDA)** in **1974**. KDA is the direct successor to the 1919 Improvement Trust. The colonial "improvement trust" did not die; it changed its name to "development authority." Same carve-out, modern paperwork.

The lesson Kanpur teaches the atlas: **devolution does not track age.** A city can be among the oldest municipalities in the country and still control only half its services, because the hollowing-out is not a colonial accident — the decisive parastatal carve-outs are *post-1960s*, and they were built on a template laid down in 1919.

## What was taken

The elected **Kanpur Nagar Nigam** still runs the visible, labour-intensive layer of the city — roads, solid waste, street lighting, storm-water drainage, parks, and public health. That is the 50%.

The other half has been routed around the council:

- **Water and sewerage → Jal Sansthan / UP Jal Nigam** — a state board, not the elected body. The most basic municipal function in a city built on the Ganga is run by the state.
- **Land and planning → KDA** — the 1919 Improvement Trust's heir, a state board.
- **The metro → UPMRC**, a special-purpose vehicle, entirely off the municipal books.
- **City bus** is a small SPV (verify). **Electricity (KESCO)** and **police** sit outside the corporation as context.

One of the oldest industrial municipalities in India — a 1861 council, a 1919 improvement trust, the very lineage Ghosh traces — and its water and its land were handed to state bodies long ago. The 74th Amendment said this would be different.
