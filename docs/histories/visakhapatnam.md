# Visakhapatnam: an old municipality that kept its water — and still doesn't own its land

**Municipality: 1858** · **Municipal Corporation: 1979** · **Greater Visakhapatnam Municipal Corporation (GVMC): 2005** · devolution score today: **73%** (8 of 11 resident-facing services run by the elected corporation).

## The dates that matter

Visakhapatnam is genuinely old — older, as a municipality, than most of the cities this atlas tracks. But age and power do not travel together, and Vizag is the clean proof.

- **1858** — Visakhapatnam becomes a municipality. It is a district town of the **Madras Presidency**, and this date sits in the same generation as Pune's municipal beginnings. A civic body that taxes the town to maintain the town exists here a full ninety years before the Republic.
- **1979** — The municipality is reconstituted as a **Municipal Corporation**. This is the gap that matters: the *municipal roots* are mid-nineteenth-century, but the *corporation form* — the full-fledged elected city government — arrives only in the late twentieth.
- **2005** — The **Greater Visakhapatnam Municipal Corporation (GVMC)** is constituted, folding surrounding areas into a single greater-city body.

A careful note, because the atlas does not flatten history: Vizag belongs to the **Madras-Presidency** lineage — the same world as Madras itself (incorporated as a corporation in 1688). But where Madras got the *corporation* form early, Vizag got it late. Old municipality, late corporation. That is the Madras-Presidency pattern outside the Presidency capital: deep municipal roots, but the modern elected city government grafted on much later.

## What was taken

Vizag scores **73%** — high for this atlas — and the reason is unusually concrete: GVMC kept **water in-house**. Where many cities have surrendered the costliest utility, here the elected corporation still runs it.

In-house, under the elected GVMC: **water, sewerage, roads, solid waste, street lighting, storm-water drainage, parks, and public health.** Eight functions, the elected body's own departments. That is the 73%.

But a high score is not a clean one, and the pattern is the one the rest of the atlas documents:

- **City bus → APSRTC** — the state road-transport corporation, not the council.
- **Land and planning → VMRDA** (Visakhapatnam Metropolitan Region Development Authority, 2017) — a state board, successor to **VUDA** (1978). The thread runs unbroken from improvement-trust logic to development-authority logic: a body that holds the city's land *outside* the elected corporation.
- **Fire → AP Fire Services** — a state department.
- No metro yet (planned). Electricity (**APEPDCL**) and police are state context.

And then the largest decision of all. The major SEZ and data-centre land — the **Atchutapuram–Rambilli** belt, the Google–Adani investment zone *(verify the specifics)* — sits with **APIIC**, the state industrial-infrastructure corporation, **entirely outside GVMC**. The elected city runs your tap and your bins. It does not get a vote on the industrial land that will define the city's next fifty years.

That is the whole indictment in one city: an old Madras-Presidency municipality that held onto its water — and so reads as relatively devolved — but whose biggest land decisions are made by a state corporation it does not control. As Ghosh's framing has it, the improvement trust became the development authority, and the development authority holds the land that the elected council never gets to touch.
