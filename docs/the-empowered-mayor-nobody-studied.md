# The empowered mayor nobody studied: M.K. Stalin, Chennai, 1996–2002

A note for the SevenT4 atlas, because it bears directly on the project's mayoral-power
thesis. The question was simple — what has been written about M.K. Stalin's tenure as
Mayor of Chennai (1996–2002), when his father M. Karunanidhi was Chief Minister of
Tamil Nadu? Three corpus + web sweeps (the user's Zotero, then two deep web sweeps of
print archives, biographies, scholarship, Shodhganga, and Tamil sources) converge on a
single, telling answer.

## The verdict: rich journalism, almost no scholarship

**The journalism is real and findable.** The mayoralty enters the national record most
sharply through the **2001 "mini-flyover scam"** and the midnight arrest of Karunanidhi
and Stalin — the legal fallout of Singara Chennai's flyover-building spree (T.S.
Subramanian, *"Tamil Nadu's Shame," Frontline* Vol. 18 No. 14, 7 July 2001). The
**Singara Chennai** ("Beautiful Chennai") programme and the **"Flyover Man"** sobriquet
recur across contemporary and retrospective press (TNM, ThePrint, Deccan Herald,
Rediff). The directly-elected-mayor experiment itself — Chennai's first directly elected
mayor in the 1996 revival election after a 1973–96 gap — is best covered journalistically
by The News Minute.

**The scholarship is thin to absent.** Across EPW, IJPA, Nagarlok, Urban India, Google
Scholar, SSRN, academia.edu, and Shodhganga, **no peer-reviewed article, book chapter, or
thesis takes the 1996–2002 Chennai mayoralty, Singara Chennai, or the direct-mayor
experiment as its subject.** The nearest scholarly item — Niraj Kumar, "Directly Elected
Mayors," *EPW* 54(46), 2019 — is national and general; it never mentions Tamil Nadu,
Chennai, or Stalin. EPW's own "Chennai" tag returns music, gender, caste, and health, but
nothing on the civic office. (Shodhganga's search UI timed out, so the thesis sweep is
indicative, not exhaustive — a TN-ULB political-science thesis with a direct-mayor chapter
plausibly exists but is poorly indexed; a manual repository search is the open follow-up.)

**Tamil and primary sources** confirm the shape: Tamil Wikipedia's M.K. Stalin entry has a
dedicated *"சென்னை மாநகராட்சி மேயர்"* (Chennai Corporation Mayor) subsection (Singara
Chennai, the flyovers, the 2001 re-election, the 2002 resignation), and the richest
primary source — **Stalin's own autobiography** (*Ungalil Oruvan / One Among You*) — has
its Vol. 1 stop at **1976**, twenty years short of the mayoralty; the volumes that would
cover it are not yet in print. The two major Karunanidhi biographies (Panneerselvan 2021;
Vaasanthi 2020) treat Stalin as *successor* and dwell on family friction, not on his
mayoral governance.

## Why this is itself a finding

India's mayors are, by design, weak and ceremonial; the rare *empowered, directly elected*
mayor is the exception that should be most studied. Stalin's was exactly that case —
twice directly elected, executive in practice (because of the dynastic link to the CM),
a launchpad to the Deputy CM's and then CM's chair — and Tamil Nadu is one of the few
states that **adopted, used, and then scrapped** direct mayoral elections. That this has
gone substantially **un-theorised** is a real gap, not a search failure: the academy
studied the *weak* mayor (the democratic-deficit literature) and the *rural* panchayat
(the decentralisation literature), and skipped the one urban executive that actually held
power.

For SevenT4 this matters twice over. First, it vindicates the atlas's focus on mayoral
power as an under-examined hinge of the 74th Amendment. Second, it is a caution: the
empowered Indian mayor we *do* have on record was made by **family, not by institution**,
and his signature record (the flyovers) ended in a corruption case — so "empower the
mayor" is not a clean prescription. The office needs studying precisely because the one
time it had teeth, the result was ambiguous and nobody examined it.
