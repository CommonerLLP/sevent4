The explanatory layer

Every city has a tracker.
None of them tell you why.

You can find your air quality, your potholes, your water bills in a dozen apps. What no one shows you is why it stays this way, year after year — who is responsible, why they don't act, and what you can actually do about it. That is the only thing worth building. This is where the atlas stops measuring the symptom and starts explaining the disease — and every chapter ties it back to the two things that are unmistakably yours: the taxes that fund it and the vote that authorises it.

How every chapter works

Four questions, one honest answer each

Each chapter takes one thing your city fails at and walks the same four steps — from the problem you live with, to the lever you actually hold.

1 · Burden
What you live with
The symptom, measured honestly — the bad air, the missing library, the broken road.
2 · Who
Who's responsible
The specific office or officer the law makes accountable — named, not abstract.
3 · Why
Why it persists
The structural reason — empty offices, no money, captured contracts, no power to tax.
4 · Act
What you can do
The real lever — organise and go public first, then arm it with the RTI, the tribunal, the vote.

The hard truth that runs through all of it: most of these offices answer to no one — no election, no constituency — which is exactly why they decay. The lever, then, is rarely a single vote and never a single form. It is organised pressure: people who turn a scattered grievance into a standing demand the office cannot ignore. Each chapter ends by handing you both — the names to target, how to organise, and the documents to arm it.
