The Unelected City

India's cities are run less by the people you vote for than by officers the state appoints — and in several of them, no one has been elected at all for years. Pick a city to see its wards, its representatives (councillor → MLA → MP), the appointed officers who actually decide, and whether its residents currently have an elected municipal government.

Coverage is being built out city by city; Ahmedabad is the most complete. A red flag means the corporation currently has no elected council — run by a state-appointed administrator. Data is a research reading of public records.

The analytical spine of this atlas — the Indian municipality as a corporate machine built to extract rent rather than deliver service — is drawn wholeheartedly from the work of the historian Anwesha Ghosh (Municipal Calcutta: Urban Governance in a Colonial City, University of Toronto, 2021). The application to contemporary cities is ours; the framework is hers.
