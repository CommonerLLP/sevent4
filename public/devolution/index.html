Part IXA · the 73rd & 74th Amendments

How much was actually devolved?

In 1992 two amendments promised to push power down from the states: the 73rd to elected village panchayats, the 74th to elected cities. But both made devolution the state's choice — Articles 243G and 243W say a state "may" hand power down. So thirty years on, the same legislature gives its villages one amount and its cities another, and a function "transferred" on paper often arrives with no money and no staff to run it. This is that gap, state by state.

What the scoreboard shows

Rank ≠ control — read the two columns against each other, never as one number.

Karnataka tops the rural index (#1, 72.23) — yet its city, Bengaluru, has had no elected government at all since 2020, run by a state-appointed IAS administrator, and sits dead last (#23) on the urban survey. A state can ace panchayat paperwork and still hollow out its cities.

Ahmedabad is more independent than Bengaluru has ever been.

Gujarat is mid-pack on the rural index (#6) — but its corporation is one of the most independent in India: the AMC runs 10 of the 18 functions in-house, raises ~50% of its own revenue (rare for an Indian city), scores ASICS #7, and — unlike Bengaluru — has a sitting elected council. On functions, on finance, and on simply having an elected city government, Ahmedabad clears Bengaluru on every de jure axis. The 2.5-year mayoral term is a footnote, not the verdict.

But de jure power ≠ de facto freedom — and here the two invert.

Devolution measures the corporation's power, not the resident's freedom. That same well-funded, autonomous Ahmedabad governs one of the most residentially segregated major cities in India. The Gujarat Disturbed Areas Act requires state permission to transfer property between Hindus and Muslims in notified zones — sold as protection against distress sales, it instead freezes the ghetto in place. Juhapura, home to up to half the city's Muslims, is hemmed by the Act and goes short on roads, drainage, water, schools and hospitals (Tejani, Urban Studies 2023) — and the AMC's own services run thinnest in exactly these segregated wards.

Run it the other way and it flips: Bengaluru — with no elected council at all — is the easier city to simply live in. A single woman, an unmarried couple, a Muslim tenant will find rental doors open in Bangalore that Ahmedabad's vegetarian-only housing societies and Act-frozen blocks keep shut. A strong local state can be a strong instrument of exclusion. Read every score on this page as de jure — then go and check it against the de facto city.

"Shared" responsibility is the failure, not a grey area.

Where the plan is the state's and the permit the city's, where the road is the city's and the bus the state's — when the lane floods or the library closes, each tier blames the other and neither answers. Diffused accountability is the mechanism by which a service fails with no one to vote out. It is a deficit, never a wash.

The 3 Fs move apart on purpose.

A function can be "devolved" while the funds stay with an irregular State Finance Commission (overdue in 8 of these 11 states) and the functionaries stay on state deputation. UP is the sharp case: a directly-elected 5-year mayor on paper, with the money and the staff withheld in fact.
