Indian cities often fail residents because responsibility is split across municipal bodies, state departments, parastatals, engineers, contractors, MLAs, MPs, and councillors. This project helps people connect a civic problem to the public geography and public office around it.

What this is

Not a smart-city dashboard.

This atlas does one small thing: it gives residents a way to ask, “whose jurisdiction is this?” Select a ward, Assembly constituency, or Parliamentary constituency; see the public services and infrastructure layers around it; then connect visible civic problems to councillors, MLAs, MPs, municipal departments, and public offices as city data improves.

Part IXA handbook

Articles residents should know

The 74th Amendment does not automatically make cities powerful. It gives a constitutional language for asking why powers, functions, and finances have not moved down to urban local bodies.

Article 243P

Definitions of municipal territory

Defines the basic vocabulary of Part IXA: municipality, municipal area, district, metropolitan area, population, and related terms. Without these definitions, city power cannot be mapped or compared.

Civic meaning

The map starts with legal boundaries.

A resident cannot attribute responsibility if the public boundary itself is unclear. Definitions are not technical trivia; they decide what counts as the city and who can be questioned.
