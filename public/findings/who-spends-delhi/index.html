Who spends Delhi's money?

Delhi has not one civic budget but three, run by three different kinds of body. Delhiites elect two of them — a state government (the Legislative Assembly) and a city corporation — and the third is nominated. Yet the tier closest to your neighbourhood, the elected city corporation, commands the smallest share; and even the elected state government doesn't control the city's police, land or public order. In Delhi, the elected layers are the ones kept either small or hemmed in.

What the comparison does — and doesn't — say

Read it carefully, because the bars are not strictly like-for-like. The GNCTD figure is the elected state government's whole budget — health, education, transport, welfare across the NCT — not just municipal services; a large part of it is state-tier spending the MCD was never meant to do. So this is not "the city spends ₹17,012 cr and someone unelected hoards the rest." Two of these three budgets are democratically elected.

What it does show is which kind of body the money tracks. The tier closest to a resident — the elected municipal corporation, the one the 74th Amendment was meant to empower — is the smallest, about one-sixth of the state government's budget. And the Delhi twist sits inside the biggest bar: even the elected GNCTD does not run the city's police, land (DDA) or public order — those are the Union's, exercised through the Lieutenant-Governor. So the money concentrates in the elected state tier, while the powers a city most visibly needs are split off above it. The elected layers are the small one and the hemmed-in one.

And the enclave the money loves

The third bar is the sharpest, because it is unelected. The NDMC — a council with no directly elected member — spends ₹5,307 cr governing the capital's richest few square miles (Lutyens', the ministries, ~258,000 residents). Meanwhile the state government's budget has ballooned from about ₹20,000 crore in 2008-09 to ₹1 lakh crore in 2025-26 — a fivefold rise — and the elected city corporation's slice has not kept pace.

So the question this finding poses is not "is Delhi underfunded," nor even "is it unelected." It is plainly neither — over a lakh crore flows through its civic bodies, and two of the three are voted in. The question is which of your votes carries weight: the municipal vote reaches the smallest budget; the Assembly vote reaches the largest, but that government is overruled on police, land and order by an officer no Delhi voter chooses. So which ballot actually decides how your city is run — and is any of them the city's own?

And the harder follow-on for "devolve more power to cities": in Delhi the elected city is the ₹17,000-crore layer while the ₹1-lakh-crore decisions sit at the state tier — itself hemmed by the Union. Power follows money and the money sits a tier (or two) above the ward. Which body would you actually have to win to change how Delhi is run?
