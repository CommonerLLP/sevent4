Part IXA · the evidence

What does the data actually show?

Each finding starts from a question a resident might ask — about power, money, or who gets served — and follows it into the public record. They are questions traced to their source, not demands. Read each score as de jure, then check it against the city you live in.

How much power was actually devolved to your city?
A per-state scoreboard of the 1992 amendments — rural Devolution Index against the urban function-count and ASICS rank — read through the gap between what a state grants on paper and what its city can do in fact.
 Ambedkar · the frame
Should your city have more power?
The atlas says your city controls little — so give it more? The man who drafted the Constitution would replace the question. His one test for devolution: can the people at the bottom be captured at this scale?
 Ambedkar · 1955
Should a city be its own state?
Ambedkar's forgotten proposal that Bombay belong to neither Maharashtra nor Gujarat but govern itself — the closest thing in Indian thought to a positive theory of the city as a polity, and what it asks of us now.
 Ambedkar · the city & caste
Why did Ambedkar trust the city?
Not as theory — as biography. The same man, a Columbia graduate, was hounded from a Baroda inn and freed in a New York classroom. His own life ran the test: could caste reach him here? The bigger the city, the less it could hold him — and what that asks of a city now being re-sorted to know exactly who you are.
 Delhi · power & the ballot
Who elects the government of New Delhi?
Parliament, the ministries, Lutyens', the richest addresses in India — all run by a municipal council with zero directly elected members, by statute. The capital's wealthiest enclave is its least democratic government. The unelected city in its purest form.
 Delhi · the money map
Who spends Delhi's money?
Delhi has three civic budgets — the elected state govt (₹1 lakh cr), the elected city corporation (₹17,012 cr), and a nominated enclave (₹5,307 cr). The tier closest to your ward holds the least; and even the elected state govt doesn't run the city's police or land. Parsed from all three bodies' own budgets.
