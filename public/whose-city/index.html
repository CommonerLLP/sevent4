The first lesson
Most people remember the party. Fewer are taught which office has the power.

That knowledge gap is not a personal failure. It is the first lesson: the city is split across ward, corporation, board, department, contractor, Assembly constituency, and Parliamentary constituency until responsibility becomes hard to name.

This is a learning exercise, not a test of intelligence. National and state voting cannot substitute for knowing who governs your street, your air, your drain, your police station, and your budget. Neutrality does not keep you outside politics; it leaves the map of power with someone else, including who benefits from that confusion, and who pays for it.

What this lesson opens

None of the taxing power, an unelected officer at the wheel, and half its services decided elsewhere. This is why local politics cannot be treated as small, dirty, or optional.

Politicization is a practice you can learn: identify the office, name the power, ask who controls each civic problem, and organise at the scale where the decision is actually made. The maps are the next lesson — who decides, who profits, who pays, who is protected, and who is made to live with the damage.
