Who controls the money

Who decided? · Who profits? · Who pays?

The Ahmedabad Metro is ~₹16,000 crore of capital — the city's single biggest mobility decision. Almost none of it is on the elected municipal corporation's books. Follow Phase 1's ₹10,773 crore: where it came from, through which vehicle, and to whom it was paid.

Source amounts are proportional. The recipient fan is un-sized: individual contract values are not all public. Gold = value paid to a foreign supplier (the Japanese loan partly returns to Japan via Nippon Signal — the tied-aid loop).

The asymmetry

Every party on the right is paid in full whether or not the trains run, and whether the project is on time or five years late. The risks — cost overrun, delay, low ridership, foreign-exchange movement, and the years of dust and congestion along the corridor — sit entirely with the public and the people who live beside the line. A delay is not a shared misfortune; it is a one-sided transfer.

Why this is the point, not a footnote

The bus the city does control — AMTS — gets about ₹525 crore a year, and Janmarg/BRTS about ₹143 crore. The Phase 1 delay alone cost an estimated ₹5,000–8,000 crore in overrun and forgone returns — a decade or more of the entire city bus programme. The most expensive mobility decision in the city is the one least accountable to the city's voters. That is the 74th-Amendment devolution gap, made into a money trail. See the 22-year city budget for the bus-funding line.
