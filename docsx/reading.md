# Reading

This atlas does not read the Indian city as a management problem to be optimised.
It reads it as a **site of caste–capital domination** — built and cleaned by Dalit,
Bahujan, and migrant labour, and governed without their governing — and asks how it
is reclaimed. The reading is ordered by that frame, not by the establishment's.

## Caste, capital, and the right to the city — the frame

- **B.R. Ambedkar** — *Writings and Speeches* (BAWS, Vasant Moon ed.): the city as
  the Dalit's escape from the village's closed caste order (Bombay as a city-state),
  the *Children of India's Ghetto* and "touching space," and the warning that the
  spirit of the Constitution can be perverted through the *form of administration*.
  *Annihilation of Caste*. The foundational text of this project.
- **Anand Teltumbde** — *Republic of Caste: Thinking Equality in the Time of
  Neoliberal Hindutva* — the Brahminism–capitalism nexus that the privatised,
  outsourced city runs on.
- **Henri Lefebvre** — *The Right to the City* (1968) — the city as collective work
  (*oeuvre*), and the right to remake urban life, not merely to access it.
- **David Harvey** — *Rebel Cities* and "The Right to the City" (NLR, 2008) —
  urbanisation as the absorption of surplus capital, and the right to the city as
  the collective power to change it.
- **Jan Breman** — *Footloose Labour* — India's informal, circulating workforce: the
  class that builds the city and is dispossessed by it.
- **Gopal Guru** — on humiliation, caste, and the politics of urban space.
- **Jayati Ghosh** and **Melinda Cooper** — on neoliberalism, labour, and the
  financialised austerity that hollows out public provision and parks it in
  fixed deposits while the work goes undone.

## The formal promise — the text to hold the state to

The 74th Amendment wrote local self-government into the Constitution and then let the
states withhold it. We read the law as the promise the city is *owed*, and measure
the de facto city against it — the gap is the argument.

- Constitution of India, **Part IXA: The Municipalities**, Articles 243P–243ZG —
  <https://www.constitutionofindia.net/parts/part-ixa/>
- **Article 243W** (powers, authority and responsibilities of municipalities) —
  <https://www.constitutionofindia.net/articles/article-243w-powers-authority-and-responsibilities-of-municipalities-etc/>
- **74th Amendment & the Twelfth Schedule** — the 18 functions the city is promised
  and rarely given —
  <https://secforuts.mha.gov.in/74th-amendment-and-municipalities-in-india/>

## The managerial frame we read *against*

These name the technocratic "good-governance" / "city-systems" discourse — the state
and its NGO–consultant ecosystem that recasts a problem of power, caste, and class as
one of efficiency, rankings, and metrics. We mine their numbers (Janaagraha's ASICS
is a source for the urban devolution scoreboard) but reject their depoliticised lens;
they are read to be dismantled, not followed.

- **NITI Aayog**, *Moving Towards Effective City Government — A Framework for
  Million-Plus Cities* — the apex neoliberal-planning body's managerial recasting of
  the city —
  <https://niti.gov.in/whats-new/moving-towards-effective-city-government-framework-million-plus-cities>
- **Janaagraha**, *Annual Survey of India's City-Systems (ASICS)* — corporate-
  philanthropy urban reformism; useful chiefly as the establishment's own catalogue
  of municipal weakness, read against its "systems" framing —
  <https://www.janaagraha.org/asics/>
