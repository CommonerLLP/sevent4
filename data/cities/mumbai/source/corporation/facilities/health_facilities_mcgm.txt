Health Services

Background:

Mumbai has a good public-health infrastructure. The health services for people in Mumbai
are met through Hospitals and dispensaries run by the Municipal Corporation of Greater
Mumbai (MCGM), Maharashtra state and the private sector. The MCGM has a network of
four medical college-Hospitals, one dental college-Hospital, 16 municipal general Hospitals,
six specialty Hospitals, 29 maternity homes, 175 municipal dispensaries and 183 health posts.
In addition, the Maharashtra state government has one medical college Hospital, three general
Hospitals and two health units located in Mumbai.

The MCGM covers an area of 434 sq km. The different health services are distributed
MCGM runs several through Municipal Hospitals, clinics and dispensaries, as the chart
below shows number of registered health care facilities in Mumbai.

According to the 2015-16 Health Profile published by the MCGM public health department,
these were the health statistics of the city:

This complex system is designed to reach the city’s residents through preventive, promotive,
and curative care. The health services are provided in multiple ways. On one hand, the
Hospitals and dispensaries are spread all over the city catering to the medical needs of the
people. At the ground level, outreach services help to emphasise preventive care like
vaccination, water sanitation and hygiene — these are run by the Departments of Preventive
and Social Medicine.

As many as 15 million patients are treated annually in the Out-Patient Departments (OPDs) in
the MCGM Hospitals and dispensaries. Each of the peripheral Hospitals is linked to one of
the four (tertiary) specialty Hospitals. The health posts and dispensaries are linked to the
peripheral Hospitals in their respective Wards. These health posts have been established to
provide convenient and easy to access locations for people seeking treatment for ailments that
do not require a visit to a major Hospital. The indoor-bed strength of the above Hospitals is
more than 12000 beds.

The MCGM Hospitals are also involved in the National Health Programs — National
Leprosy Eradication program, National Malaria Control program, Family Welfare and
Maternal Child Health, AIDS control program, and School Health Program. For the
economically challenged people, the government has come up with many schemes viz; Rajiv
Gandhi Jeevandayee Arogya Yojana & Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram.

Services

The teaching Hospitals and the specialized Hospitals provide basic as well as tertiary-level
care. Peripheral Hospitals are designed to cater to primary and secondary care. At these
places, serious cases are recognised and are further referred to the tertiary Hospitals.

Progress

The MCGM has plans to make its healthcare functioning paperless over the next few years.
Each patient will be allotted a unique identification number when he/she visits the Hospital
for the first time. Thereafter, all case papers, test results, and diagnostic imaging results will
be stored in a centralised computerised system. This is to avoid duplication of papers, as well
as inconvenience to the patients, who have to carry their records from one department to
other within a Hospital or between two or more Hospitals.
