# The unelected city is a built city: how urban labour is extracted from

## The frame: Harvey's secondary circuit, Ambedkar's division of labourers

The corporate city (see [`the-corporate-city.md`](../../docs/the-corporate-city.md)) is built to
take rent, not give service. This file asks the next question: *whose bodies pay for the building and
the running?* Two lenses answer it together. **David Harvey** locates the city in capital's
**secondary circuit** — when surplus cannot be profitably reinvested in production, it floods the
**built environment** (real estate, roads, infrastructure) as a sink. That circuit runs on a cheap,
mobile, disposable construction proletariat, and it is sustained by a second moment Harvey calls
**accumulation by dispossession**: the worker who builds the city is dispossessed *again* in the
sphere of reproduction, made to buy water, shelter and credit back at predatory prices. **Ambedkar**
supplies the missing axis. In *Annihilation of Caste* the caste "division of labour" is also a
"**division of labourers**" — graded, hereditary, unequal. India's urban labour hierarchy is that
graded division made into a labour market: OBC/SC/Adivasi/Muslim migrants in construction, Valmiki
Dalits in sanitation and sewer death, Dalits on the head-load. The reserve army has a caste.

## What the evidence shows

**Construction: the disposable proletariat of the secondary circuit.** Construction is India's
**second-largest employer** — ~5 crore workers on the Ministry of Labour's own 2020 estimate, ~81%
unskilled — and the single largest absorber of internal migrants, who form an estimated 50-60% of the
trade. It is fed by **circular migration** from a fixed set of poor states: Bihar, UP, Odisha,
Jharkhand, West Bengal, Chhattisgarh, with ~100 million people estimated in circular movement. It is
also the **deadliest** sector: the British Safety Council figure of ~38 fatal construction accidents a
day circulated in 2022-23, with construction contributing ~24% of India's ~48,000 estimated annual
occupational deaths — and every source stresses the real toll is higher because site deaths go
unrecorded. This is the reserve army in operation: oversupplied, undocumented, interchangeable.

**The BOCW cess: the strongest documented case in the file.** Parliament was told that as on 1 Nov
2022, state Building & Other Construction Workers' Welfare Boards had **collected Rs 87,478.79 crore**
in cess and spent Rs 49,269.20 crore — leaving **Rs 38,209.59 crore unspent** (~44%). This is not a
one-year glitch: in 2016 only ~21% had been spent; by Feb 2022, ~55% of Rs 78,521 crore sat
unutilised. The Supreme Court, in **National Campaign Committee for Central Legislation on
Construction Labour v. Union of India**, recorded >Rs 37,400 crore collected against ~Rs 9,500 crore
used, called the facts "startling," and (19 Mar 2018) ordered a model scheme and social audits. The
state taxes every building to fund the workers who build it — and then sits on the money, blaming
"low worker registration" it controls, while the CAG finds chunks diverted to populist schemes
(Rs 416 crore in MP). Only **Kerala** spends >90%. A statutory fund, levied on the worker's labour,
withheld from the worker: accumulation by dispossession in plain accounting.

**The contractor chain dilutes the wage and dissolves the employer.** Recruitment runs
principal-employer → labour contractor → petty contractor → **jamadar/sardar/maistry**, who recruits
whole families from villages against a **monetary advance** — the seed of debt-bondage that ties the
worker for a >6-month season. The jamadar, given money for the workers' food and shelter, returns only
part of it. With **no written contract**, liability is pushed down and out of reach: minimum wage,
BOCW registration and accident compensation become unenforceable. It is the same two-tier structure
the atlas documents in municipal sanitation — secure tier above precarious tier, identical work,
sub-half wages — only deeper, and migrant.

**The pothole economy.** CAG audits document the contractor-engineer nexus directly: collusive
bidding "in most districts," contractors below technical thresholds qualified, "undue benefits...
rampant," ~Rs 40,000 crore of UP road works (2011-16) failing before design life, 85 of 92 Kerala
projects passed without mandatory field labs. Engineers pre-announce inspections; private auditors
clear substandard work for payment; **no contractor is blacklisted**. Read with Ghosh, the road that
must be rebuilt every monsoon is not a failure — it is a contract that renews.

**The new contractor is an algorithm.** Gig/platform work (NITI Aayog: 77 lakh in 2020-21, heading to
2.35 crore by 2029-30) reproduces the contractor chain digitally. Rajasthan (2023) and Karnataka
(2025) legislated welfare cesses (1-5% of each transaction) — real, but they largely dodge the
**classification** fight: the worker stays an "independent partner," bearing fuel and risk, controlled
by the app. The algorithm is the new jamadar.

**Secondary extraction in reproduction.** The worker is extracted from again in consumption. In
Mumbai's informal settlements, tanker water cost a median ~Rs 135/1,000 litres — **40x the municipal
rate** — and non-notified slum dwellers pay up to 50x, because the state declines to pipe the colonies
the tanker "mafia" then serves by siphoning the public network. Predatory pricing is *enabled* by
deliberate non-service.

**Caste runs top to bottom.** e-Shram's 7.86 crore informal workers are ~72.5% SC/ST/OBC. Sanitation
is ~77% Dalit (NAMASTE); manual scavengers ~97% SC; and the **1,313 official sewer/septic deaths
(1993-2025)** — double that by SKA's count — fall almost entirely on contract/informal Dalit men, not
permanent staff. Ambedkar's graded division of labourers is the organising principle of the whole
extractive machine: the city is built and cleaned by the castes it was built to keep below.
