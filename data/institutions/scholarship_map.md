# Scholarly grounding & further reading: the literature behind *The Unelected City*

This is the atlas's reading layer — the scholarship on Indian urban governance read *through*
Anwesha Ghosh's frame (municipality-as-corporation, municipal governmentality, the
improvement-trust-to-development-authority lineage, recoupment / land financialisation, the
fractured public). Entries are tagged `source:"zotero"` where they sit in the project corpus and
`source:"web/knowledge"` where the work is real and load-bearing for Ghosh's argument but not held
as a standalone record here. Organised by city; honest gaps flagged at the end.

## Kolkata / Calcutta — the home ground
Ghosh's own two works anchor everything: the thesis *Municipal Calcutta* (2021) and the article
**"The Improvement Regime"** (2025, *CSSH*), which extends the argument to a Bombay–Calcutta
comparison and supplies the word "recoupment." The deep context is **Debjani Bhattacharyya,
*Empire and Ecology in the Bengal Delta* (2018)** — how colonial law dried Calcutta's wetlands into
speculable property, the property-making Ghosh's municipality both performs and preys on.
**Partho Datta**'s *Planning the City* (the source of Ghosh's "devolution as travesty" quotation)
and **Nabaparna Ghosh**'s *A Hygienic City-Nation* (2020, the caste/bhadralok underside of
"improvement") are cited throughout Ghosh but are *not* standalone records in the corpus — a gap.

## Bombay / Mumbai — the strongest non-Calcutta file
Two registers, both well covered. For the **colonial improvement trust**, **Prashant Kidambi**'s
work (here, "Planning, the information order, and the Bombay census of 1901," 2013; plus his
*Making of an Indian Metropolis* and "Housing the Poor," cited via Ghosh and McGowan) is the
keystone — the BIT (1898) as the first Indian planning-agency carve-out. For the **contemporary
parastatal**, **Nikhil Anand's *Hydraulic City* (2017)** and his article cluster (*Pressure* 2011,
*Municipal Disconnect* 2012, *Leaky States* 2015, *Consuming Citizenship* 2020) are the richest
single body of work in the whole map: water as differentiated citizenship, the utility as a
selective provider — the fractured public in pipes. **Zainab Bawa** (2011) adds the councillor's
limited mediating reach; **Sapana Doshi**'s "Redevelopmental State" gives the land-dispossession
machine its contemporary theory; and **Juned Shaikh's *Outcaste Bombay* (2021)** supplies the
caste dimension — planners marking Dalit neighbourhoods as demolishable "slums."

## Chennai / Madras — strong on water, thin on the colonial corporation
**Karen Coelho**'s Metrowater ethnography (2004 thesis) is the definitive account of the
neoliberal water parastatal — audit culture, the engineer displaced by the accountant, hostility to
"over-politicized" slum-dwellers: Ghosh's "trusting the experts" in the field. **Niranjana R.**
(2021) and the Coelho-et-al. **Ennore Creek** counter-mapping piece (2024) extend it. For the
*colonial* Madras corporation the corpus leans on **Bhavani Raman's *Document Raj* (2012)** (law,
petition, property-making) and **Frykenberg** (roads/riots and the contested "public"); **Aparna
Balachandran**'s work on the Madras corporation and the "trader-governor" — which Ghosh explicitly
invokes — is referenced but not held here. **Catriona Ellis**'s Madras midday-meals study is a
useful *counter-case*: an elected municipality doing welfare.

## Bengaluru / Bangalore — strong on the speculative present, the canonical past
**Janaki Nair's *Promise of the Metropolis* (2005)** is the standard 20th-century city-biography.
The contemporary turn is well stocked: **Carol Upadhya** (software/liberalisation and land
conflict), **Hemangini Gupta** (*Experimental Times*, startup speculation as governance),
**Gidwani & Reddy** ("wasteland" accumulation / eviscerating urbanism), **Nafis Aziz Hasan**
(Bhoomi land records as property-making), and **Shreyas Sreenath** ("Numbing Machines," caste in
the sewer). The one named gap: **Michael Goldman's "speculative urbanism"** articles — cited
*within* the corpus (Hasan, Doshi reference "Goldman 2011/2020") but not held as standalone records.

## Hyderabad — single but well-aimed
**Sangeeta Kamat** (2011) is the load-bearing work: Hyderabad remade as an IT "global city" and the
**Hyderabad Metropolitan Development Authority** redrawing the city's boundaries — the
development-authority carve-out, Telangana edition. Otherwise thin.

## Ahmedabad — the atlas's primary city, decently covered
**Mona Mehta, "The Middle Class Megacity" (2016)** is exactly on-frame: a middle class realising its
interests "without depending on electoral democracy." **Arvind Rajagopal** (planning as communal
segregation), **Abigail McGowan** (housing politics 1920–60, a briefly *inclusive* municipal
history), and **Rakib Akhtar** (the Hindutva-neoliberal Gujarat model) round it out.

## Delhi — the comparator, with the cleanest lineage
**Stephen Legg's** "Post-Colonial Developmentalities" traces the **Delhi Improvement Trust (1937) →
Delhi Development Authority** continuity verbatim — the single best illustration of Ghosh's
colonial-to-postcolonial argument. **Awadhendra Sharan** (water and "improvement") and **Ravi
Sundaram**'s *Pirate Modernity* (the informal city that exceeds planning — a useful *contrast* to
Ghosh's institution-centred reading) complete the set.

## Where the corpus is thin (honest gaps)
1. **Hyderabad** rests on essentially one work; no colonial-municipal or parastatal history of the
   GHMC/HMWSSB.
2. **Madras as a colonial corporation** is covered only obliquely; Balachandran is not in-corpus.
3. Several keystones Ghosh herself relies on are cited but **not standalone records**: Partho Datta,
   Nabaparna Ghosh, Goldman's speculative-urbanism articles, Kidambi's *Making of an Indian
   Metropolis*. Acquiring these would harden the map.
4. **Caste/labour/sanitation** is present (Shaikh, Sreenath, Lee, plus EPW sanitation pieces) but
   under-integrated into the city files — it is the axis Ghosh's frame most underweights.
5. No dedicated **municipal-finance / own-revenue** scholarship per city — the property-tax fiscal
   spine Ghosh foregrounds is the atlas's data layer, not yet its reading layer.
