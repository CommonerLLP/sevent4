# The robot and the pit: mechanisation, caste, and who still dies in the sewer

## The brutal frame

In June 2025 the Minister of State for Social Justice told Parliament that "No death
has been reported due to Manual Scavenging" (Outlook India). In the same window the
government's own National Commission for Safai Karamcharis recorded **1,313 sewer and
septic-tank deaths between 1993 and 2025**, and **377 in "hazardous cleaning" between
2019 and 2023** (groundXero; data.gov.in). Both statements are official. They are
reconciled by a single move — **the definitional dodge**. "Manual scavenging" (handling
human excreta from dry latrines) is declared eradicated; therefore there are no
manual-scavenging deaths. The men who die in sewers and septic tanks die under a
different legal heading — "hazardous cleaning" — and so do not count. The category gap
is the cover. The dead are the same caste of men, in the same gas-filled pits.

The law is unambiguous. The **Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers and their
Rehabilitation Act, 2013** bans hazardous manual cleaning of sewers and septic tanks
without protective gear. *Safai Karamchari Andolan v. Union of India* (2014) fixed
**Rs 10 lakh** compensation per death. *Dr Balram Singh v. Union of India* (2023 INSC
950) **raised it to Rs 30 lakh** (Rs 10–20 lakh for disability) and **mandated
mechanisation** — the only exception being a sewer not yet operational (LiveLaw). On
**29 January 2025** the Supreme Court ordered an end to manual sewer cleaning in **six
metros — Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Kolkata, Bengaluru, Hyderabad** (Verdictum; CJP). Five
are our cities. Ahmedabad was not named.

## The mechanisation map: a lead, a patchy middle, a manual tail

**Kerala is the lead.** The Kerala Water Authority was the first body in India to use
robotic scavengers — the **Bandicoot**, built by the Kerala-origin firm **Genrobotics** —
from Thiruvananthapuram (2018) outward, toward a "manhole-free" state (KWA; Business
Standard 2023). The robot's "Tron unit" enters the manhole so no man does.

Among **our** cities, **Chennai** is the strongest case: **CMWSSB** deployed Bandicoot
Mobility+ from April 2024 and cleaned **5,000+ manholes** in its first year (Construction
World; DQIndia). **Ahmedabad** has genuine units — **4 Bandicoots in the city** (plus
3 in Surat, 2 in Gandhinagar), 9 more announced in October 2024 under Gujarat's "Year of
Urban Development" (Bilkul Online) — but at pilot-plus scale against a vast manhole stock.
**Hyderabad** runs an earlier generation: **HMWSSB's mini-jetting/desludging trucks**
since 2017 — mechanised desludging, not robotic arms (The News Minute). **Mumbai** (a
2-robot BMC pilot) and **Bengaluru** (BWSSB's early robotic hiring) are **pilot-only**.
**Kolkata** and **Delhi** are the least mechanised in practice — Delhi despite repeated
procurement, because its work is contractor-routed and uncovered.

And yet **every one of them still kills**. Mechanising the corporation's public manholes
does not touch the **private septic tanks and apartment STPs** where sub-contracted Dalit
men still descend. Citizen Matters documents apartment STPs as a structural cause of
*continuing* manual scavenging in Bengaluru that public-manhole robots never reach. The
robot cleans the municipal hole; the worker still dies in the private tank next door.

## The data contest

**Official** (NCSK/Ministry): 54–55 deaths in 2024, ~46 in 2025. **Independent** (Safai
Karmachari Andolan / **Bezwada Wilson**): **116 in 2024, 121 in 2025**, and 41 in the
first ~80 days of 2026 — roughly **double** (Maktoob; Countercurrents). Wilson: "many
cases are overlooked or not properly recorded." **Delhi is the worst-burden metro** —
most deaths in 2025, most families still uncompensated (The Patriot); the Rs 30 lakh
that *Balram Singh* mandated routinely goes unpaid.

## Caste, and the Ambedkarite test

The dead are **overwhelmingly SC, predominantly Valmiki** — Arunthathiyar in Tamil Nadu,
Madiga/Mala in Telangana, Hela in the north. NAMASTE profiling: **~68.9% SC, ~77% Dalit,
~92% SC/ST/OBC**; manual scavengers ~97% SC (The News Minute 2024). They are almost never
the permanent, mixed-caste municipal cadre. They are **contract and informal** Dalit men —
the precise layer that contracting strips of training, PPE and accountability
(see `contractualization_map.json`). This is **Ambedkar's** *Annihilation of Caste* made
literal: the "division of labour" is a "**division of labourers**," graded and hereditary.

Here is the sharp nuance the project must hold. **Mechanisation liberates only if it ends
the caste-assignment of the work.** Hyderabad is the warning: three years after the
machines arrived, Scroll found "sickness and degradation endure" — the **same Dalit men**,
now operating machines on the same precarious terms. A Bandicoot operated by a Valmiki
worker rebadged "robot operator" on a labour contract is caste in a new shell. The machine
can protect the corporation's *liability* more reliably than the worker's *life*. The real
test — the one *Balram Singh* and Ambedkar both point to — is **permanent, dignified,
non-caste-marked municipal jobs**: the robot run by a public technician hired without
regard to jati, not a hereditary cleaner handed a new tool and the old place.
