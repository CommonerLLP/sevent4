# The Ahmedabad pothole economy: roads as a rent

The double-extraction reading (`the-double-extraction.md`) calls road contracts *the purest case*: "recurring (build, dig up, rebuild), opaque, and quality-indifferent, because bad work *guarantees the next tender*." Ahmedabad is where that sentence is documented.

## The cycle, in AMC's own numbers (tier A)

The Amdavad Municipal Corporation spends on the order of **Rs 1,000 crore a year** building, resurfacing and patching roads; the FY2025-26 budget earmarked roughly **Rs 1,200 crore for ~550 km** of new TP road and resurfacing within a Rs 14,001 crore total. The network is **~2,500-2,580 km of bituminous road** served by only **~940 km of stormwater drainage** — so the water has nowhere to go, the same stretches dissolve every monsoon, and they are patched and re-tarred again. The churn metric: ~**Rs 50 crore over four years on just 363 potholes**; **Rs 1.20 crore in a single month** (Sept 2024) on 44 potholes; one spell left **220 roads dug up and 105 areas flooded**. AMC has no policy to pre-empt potholes from drainage/manhole failure, and repairs lag 10-15 days. Gujarat Samachar's own framing is blunt: the Rs 1,000 crore is spent "by municipal authority, **contractors and politicians**."

## The flagship quality failure: Hatkeshwar (tier A)

The **Hatkeshwar flyover**, built by **Ajay Engineering Infrastructure Pvt Ltd** (consultant **SGS India Pvt Ltd**) at **~Rs 34 crore** and opened in 2017, had deteriorated so badly it was shut by 2022 — a structure meant to last decades, gone in roughly four years. **Four independent labs** (IIT Roorkee, Ecube, KCT, CMAC) found it substandard. In April 2023 Commissioner M. Thennarasan ordered demolition and reconstruction; AMC **blacklisted both the contractor and the consultant**, filed a **police complaint naming seven individuals** from the two firms, and resolved to **levy the demolition + rebuild cost on the contractor**. The demolition alone was re-tendered at ~Rs 9.31 crore. This is the strongest documented "failure-before-design-life" finding in AMC road/bridge work.

And it is also the documented proof of the mechanism. Per reporting, the same Ajay entity continued to draw AMC bridge work — **~Rs 26 crore more on the Pallav flyover** — while of a **~Rs 2.37 crore delay penalty proposed, only ~Rs 23.69 lakh was actually recovered**, with the audit also flagging bills paid before completion and weak material oversight. The firm whose structure failed kept getting paid, and kept getting work.

## The documented repeat / punished road firms (tier A)

On AMC's **own published blacklist register** (to 04-Jan-2023), of 70+ historical and ~40 current punished-contractor entries across all departments, the **only entries flagged for road quality** are two Road Project (RP) firms — **Akash Infra Project Pvt Ltd** and **GPC Infra Pvt Ltd** — under resolution 597 (29-Aug-2017), with the note that within the **Defect Liability Period** they must "complete the demaged [damaged] Road work, otherwise may declare permanent Black List." AMC's own paper thus records road work failing inside its warranty window. Separately, a **Local Fund / municipal audit** of the Omkar (Memco) railway overbridge found overhead paid at 5% and profit at 10% against a 3% tender cap, a Rs 19.34 lakh barricade payment, market-rate (not SOR) material billing, and missing steel/cement bills (DNA Ahmedabad).

## The mechanism and the limit (tier C, and tier B attributed)

Resurfacing is the ideal rent vehicle: **recurring** (the monsoon renews demand), **opaque** (buried bitumen thickness is unverifiable after laying), and **incentive-indifferent to quality** (failure produces the next patch, the next tender, the next pothole bill). The defect-liability period exists to break this — AMC's "complete the demaged Road work" note shows it *can* bite — but Hatkeshwar shows how weakly the cost is clawed back. The **corporator-contractor-engineer cartel** framing stays strictly **tier B / attributed**: opposition corporators allege "flawed execution by contractors" and critics allege deliberate poor work and repeat-L1 capture, but **no AMC or audit document cited here names a cartel or proves rigging**, and AMC and the firms reject it. The documentable core is not a proven crime; it is the lawful machine — Rs 1,000 crore a year, a road network drowning on a third of the drainage it needs, and a register on which road failures almost never end in a permanent bar.
