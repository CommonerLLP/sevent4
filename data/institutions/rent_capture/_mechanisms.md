# Follow the money: how the unelected city turns land into rent

This file documents the **shared mechanisms** by which urban land and infrastructure are converted into private gain across Kolkata, Chennai, Bengaluru and Hyderabad. The documentable, analytical core is **land-value capture**: the public assembly of land (cheaply, often coercively), its servicing with public infrastructure, and its release to the market at a multiple — with the uplift accruing to whoever controls the moment of release. This is Anwesha Ghosh's **"recoupment"** (the Calcutta Improvement Trust, 1911, financing itself by selling redeveloped surplus land at "a profit to a future development that might or might not be realized") running a century on, through the development authorities that are the improvement trust reborn: WBHIDCO/KMDA, CMDA, BDA, HMDA.

## The mechanism stack

1. **Land-value capture via development authorities.** The authority acquires agricultural/peri-urban land under public-purpose powers (the colonial Land Acquisition Act, 1894, then the 2013 LARR), services it, and releases it. The spread between farm-gate compensation and serviced-plot price is the rent. Rajarhat (≈7,500 acres for a "public" township that became corporate real estate) and Hyderabad's Kokapet/Budvel auctions are the two poles of the same move — coercive assembly at one end, headline monetisation at the other.

2. **FSI/TDR markets.** Floor-space index and transferable development rights turn the *right to build* into a tradable asset. The state manufactures scarcity (low base FSI) then sells relief (premium FSI, TDR), capturing — or letting intermediaries capture — the air above the plot.

3. **Public-land auctions / monetisation.** The cleanest, most *documented* form: e-auction of state-held plots. Tier-A by construction — the prices are published. HMDA's Neopolis (Kokapet) and Budvel auctions are the headline cases (Rs 100+ crore/acre). This is also the fiscal pivot Melinda Cooper names: the **austerity-extravagance** couplet — a state that pleads no money for services funds itself by selling the commons, often to service **revenue bonds** raised against that very land.

4. **SPV land — Smart City, metro real-estate.** Special-purpose vehicles (off the municipal books, unelected) hold land around stations and corridors. The metro's value is less the fare-box than the real-estate uplift along the line — value-capture financing made explicit.

5. **PPP concessions.** Build-operate-transfer deals bundle land with the asset; the concessionaire captures development rights as part of the bargain (the L&T Hyderabad Metro PPP carried real-estate rights; see privatization_map.json).

6. **Mega-events as land-assembly justification.** G20, National Games, "world-class city" branding supply the *depoliticised* pretext — Ghosh's "improvement" as technocratic cover — to assemble, clear and re-zone land at speed, outside ordinary deliberation.

7. **The contractor–politician nexus.** Discretionary allotment quotas, denotification (de-acquiring already-acquired land back to private hands), and rigged auctions are where capture becomes *alleged criminality*. These are **tiered carefully** below: audited findings (CAG) are Tier-A; FIRs and commission inquiries naming individuals are Tier-B reported allegations, with denials noted.

8. **Foreign capital.** JICA loans (the metros), REITs (which securitise the IT-corridor office stock built on this land), and sovereign-wealth/PE money provide the exit liquidity — the global bid that makes domestic land-value capture bankable.

## The frame

Read with Ghosh, the development authority is a **rent machine** that offers service only where it pays; the elected corporation is left "holding the broom." Read with Cooper, the public-land auction is the fiscal counterrevolution in miniature: the state declares scarcity (austerity) for water, health and housing, then stages **extravagance** — record-breaking land sales and bond-funded corridors — to recycle the commons into private balance sheets and public debt service. The city is run profitably; the question the atlas forces is *for whom*.
