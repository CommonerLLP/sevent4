# User charges, development charges and the regressive fee-shift — who bills you, and on whose authority

This dossier reads the other end of the municipal ledger from the property tax: not what the city *taxes*
but what it *charges* — water, sewerage, solid waste, development and building fees, and the state levies
that ride on the same household. The pattern is Cooper's exactly: a city starved of devolved funds (the
74th Amendment's discretionary "may, by law, endow," Art. 243W) leans not on progressive property tax but
on **regressive user fees, development charges and revenue-bond collateral** — billed, in half our cities,
by a board the resident never elected.

## The sharpest variable: who bills you for water

The carve-out is felt every month in the water bill.
- **Elected-corporation cities — Ahmedabad, Mumbai, Kolkata.** AMC and KMC fold water into the property tax;
  Mumbai's BMC bills a metered municipal tariff. The resident pays the body they elected. Kolkata is the
  outlier of outliers — domestic water is effectively **free** (a nominal fee was scrapped in 2002–03),
  pro-resident and least cost-recovering.
- **Carve-out cities — Chennai, Bengaluru, Hyderabad.** The water bill comes from a **state board the
  resident did not elect** — CMWSSB (1978), BWSSB (1964), HMWSSB (1989). The 1899/1978/1989 statutes that
  hived water out of the corporation are not an abstraction; they are the letterhead on the bill.

Bengaluru sharpens it to absurdity: as of 2025 the resident is billed by an **unelected board (BWSSB)** for
water *and* has **no elected corporation** to appeal to (BBMP dissolved, GBA elections pending). The bill
arrives from one unelected body, the complaint goes to another.

## The regressive shift, in three moves

1. **Flat and size-proxy charges replace ability-to-pay.** Chennai's CMWSSB levies a **flat ₹105/month per
   dwelling unit, unmetered** — a head charge unlinked to consumption or income. Bengaluru's BBMP **garbage
   cess (Apr 2025)** scales by *built-up area* (₹10 to ₹400/month) — a house-size proxy for a public-health
   function that used to be tax-funded. These are textbook regressive user fees.
2. **Tariff-setting is depoliticised — put on autopilot.** Mumbai's BMC has a standing resolution permitting
   an **automatic up-to-8% annual** water hike; Bengaluru's BWSSB built in an **automatic 3% annual
   escalator** from FY2026-27. The rise is insulated from any fresh political vote — Cooper's "constitutional
   austerity" in miniature: the bill goes up on a rule, beyond democratic reach.
3. **Fees pledged to debt — the resident as collateral.** Bengaluru's BDA is levying **betterment charges of
   ₹43–₹1,146 per sq ft** across 22 villages, targeting **~₹3,000 crore partly to repay a ~$3.27 bn
   Business-Corridor loan**, plus a proposed **1% corridor stamp-duty cess.** This is precisely Cooper's
   "revenue bonds and user fees as collateral": the resident's fee mortgaged to service infrastructure debt.

## The development charge: recoupment, reborn as a fee schedule

The **development/betterment charge** is Ghosh's improvement-trust *recoupment* turned into a standing tariff
— and in every city it is billed by the **unelected development authority** (AUDA, MMRDA/MCGM premiums, KMDA,
CMDA, BDA, HMDA), not the elected corporation. Mumbai is the purest land-value-capture machine of the six:
**FSI premium monetisation** ("fungible FSI" at ~50% of the ready-reckoner rate, plus premiums on staircases
and lift-lobbies) — every built-up component sold back to the builder. Kolkata's version literally *is* the
trust: KMDA **absorbed the Calcutta Improvement Trust in 2017**, so the recoupment fee now flows through the
body that inherited Ghosh's CIT.

## The biggest extraction bypasses the city entirely

**Stamp duty + registration** — the single largest one-time charge on a resident (~5% Karnataka/Gujarat,
~6–7% Maharashtra/WB/Telangana, **~11% Tamil Nadu, among India's highest**) — goes to the **state exchequer,
not the municipal corporation** that provides the services the property consumes. The city is starved while
the state taxes the city's own property market. **Professional tax** (capped at ₹2,500/yr under Art. 276) is
flat and regressive by design.

## The synthesis

The resident pays **more**, to bodies they **did not elect** (water boards, development authorities), on
**autopilot** (automatic escalators), increasingly **pledged to debt** (betterment-as-collateral) — while
the biggest property levy (stamp duty) bypasses the municipality for the state. It is austerity for the
resident and the **user fee as the new, regressive, depoliticised tax**: the elected corporation is bypassed
both in *collection* (the boards bill you) and in the *biggest take* (stamp duty goes to the state). The
household funds the unelected city, monthly.
