# The under-taxed city — the critical literature on India's property tax

*Companion to `property_tax_regimes.json` (the per-city rates/assessment systems). This dossier is the
**commentary about** property tax: why the most local, most progressive-capable tax a city owns is the one
it least collects, and who benefits from that.*

## The diagnosis, up front: 0.1–0.2% of GDP, against 1–3%

Every authoritative body that has measured it reaches the same number. India raises only about
**0.1–0.2% of GDP** from property tax. The **World Bank (2020)** puts it at **~0.2% — one-sixth of the
~1.1% OECD average**, and against **~3% in Canada, the UK and the USA**, where property tax is the bedrock
of local government [A]. The **RBI Report on Municipal Finances (2024)** is sharper still: property tax is
**~0.12% of GDP (0.15% with all municipalities), versus ~1.7% in advanced economies** [A, verify]. The
**IMF (Norregaard, 2013)** confirms this is the global pattern — ~1% of GDP in the OECD, often ~0.1–0.2% in
developing economies — and frames property tax as among the **most efficient and most under-exploited** of
all taxes [A]. NIPFP's own potential estimate (Mathur et al, 2009) was **0.16–0.24% of GDP** — meaning India
collects below even its modest measured potential [A].

The shortfall is not the rate. It is three administrative failures, named identically across the literature:

1. **Coverage.** 25–30% of properties are simply **not on the roll** (World Bank: cadasters 64–81%
   complete). RBI 2024 found **17% of 232 Municipal Corporations had coverage ratios below 20%** — fewer than
   one in five properties paying [A].
2. **Under-valuation.** Frozen Annual Rental Value rolls, decades out of date, manipulated downward;
   unauthorised floors and extensions still taxed on the original sanctioned plan (Bengaluru) [A/B].
3. **Collection.** Actual collection is **5–20% of potential** (World Bank: Bengaluru 16%, Jaipur 5%);
   collection efficiency is **30–60% of demand**; Karnataka's average is ~62% (NIPFP) [A].

Property tax is nonetheless **>60% of own tax revenue** of municipal corporations (RBI 2024) — so this one
under-collected tax is most of what a city independently earns. ASICS (Janaagraha, 2023) found cities
self-generate only **~37% of what they spend** [A]; HPEC (2011) called Indian ULBs **"among the weakest in
the world"** at raising resources while estimating a **~Rs 39 lakh crore** urban infrastructure gap [A].

## The authoritative reports (the four to cite)

- **World Bank, *Property Taxation in India* (2020)** — the cleanest single source: the 0.2%-vs-1.1%-vs-3%
  comparison, the coverage/collection figures above, and the flagship recommendation of a **state-level
  Municipal Revenue Board** to centralise GIS-based property registers across ULBs [A].
- **RBI, *Report on Municipal Finances* (2022 first edition; 2024)** — the official, recurring diagnosis;
  the 60%-of-own-tax-revenue and sub-20%-coverage findings; municipal revenue ~1% of GDP vs Brazil ~7%,
  South Africa ~6% [A].
- **NIPFP studies** (Rao, O.P. Mathur, Bandyopadhyay; WP 13-114, WP 06-39) — the academic foundation:
  potential estimates, the coverage-and-collection causal frame, and the fiscal-gap finding that most ULBs
  cannot meet expenditure needs even at full revenue potential [A].
- **15th Finance Commission (2021)** — the policy lever: **Rs 4,36,361 crore** in local grants made
  **conditional** on states notifying **minimum property-tax floor rates** (from 2022-23) and on PT
  collection growth matching the state's **5-year average GSDP growth** (from 2023-24) [A]. The strongest
  central attempt yet to force property-tax effort.

## The reform debates

The valuation-method fight runs **ARV → Unit Area Value → Capital Value System (CVS)**. ARV (annual rental
value) is the legacy method — discretionary, gameable, frozen by rent control. **Unit Area Value** (Delhi,
Bengaluru SAS since 2008) ties tax to area × zonal rate, improving transparency and buoyancy but still
leaking through coverage gaps. **CVS** (Mumbai, since 2010) pegs tax to market/ready-reckoner value — the
most progressive but the most litigated: the **Bombay High Court (24 April 2019)**, upheld by the
**Supreme Court (Nov 2022)**, struck down CVS Rules 20/21/22 for taxing future development potential, costing
BMC ~Rs 2,300 crore in adjustments [A]. The technology frontier is **GIS/satellite/drone mapping and
self-assessment**: Bengaluru's **GEPTIS** served notices to **78,524 undervalued properties** and is
GPS-tagging ~25 lakh — and met backlash and court stays [B]. The recurring institutional proposal is the
**State Property Tax / Municipal Revenue Board** (World Bank, NIPFP) to de-politicise valuation.

## The political economy: under-assessment as subsidy

This is where the atlas frame closes. Under-assessment is **not neutral inefficiency — it is a transfer**.
The World Bank notes that "creating the legal basis for property taxes goes against landed interests";
the incomplete, undervalued roll **"effectively creates an implicit subsidy for property owners"** [A]. As
urbanisation drives land values up, property-tax revenue *lags* — the gain is captured privately. The
beneficiary is the **wealthy landowner and the builder–politician nexus**: those with the most valuable
property are the most under-assessed and the best able to fight revaluation through litigation (Mumbai CVS)
and stay orders (Bengaluru GEPTIS).

This is **Cooper's counterrevolution in fiscal form**: the *progressive, ability-to-pay* property tax is
suppressed, while *regressive user fees, development charges and stamp duties* rise on autopilot to fill the
gap (see `user_fees_notes.md`). It is **Ghosh's recoupment** inverted — the city declines to tax the land
value it creates, then bills the resident for the services instead. And the burden, when it lands, is
**empirically regressive** (Mishra 2022; World Bank incidence studies) [A]: the under-taxed luxury flat and
the over-billed informal household sit on the same ledger. The un-collected property tax is the quiet engine
of the rent-capture machine — a standing public subsidy to the speculator, paid for by the user fee.
