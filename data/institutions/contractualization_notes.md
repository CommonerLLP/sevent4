# The hollow GREEN cell: contractualisation of core municipal functions

## The framework: the core-vs-auxiliary line

The devolution scorecard codes a service GREEN ("corporation") when the elected
municipality nominally runs it. But "the corporation runs it" and "the corporation's own
permanent staff operate it" are two different claims. This map measures the gap between them.

The normative line is not anti-contracting. Contracting out **auxiliary/support** functions
(IT and e-gov platforms, fleet maintenance, office security, printing, specialist
consultancy) is defensible, and so is contracting the one-off **BUILD** phase of capital
works — building a flyover is not the same as outsourcing road maintenance forever. What
hollows the elected body is contracting the **OPERATION of a core public service**: the
daily distribution of water, the sweeping of streets, the lifting of waste, the cleaning of
sewers. When that operation is handed to a private concessionaire — or, more pervasively, to
a contract/casual workforce doing the identical job beside permanent staff under the same
municipal supervisors — the corporation becomes a **contract-administering shell** even where
the statute says it "runs" the service. The GREEN cell is real on the org chart and hollow on
the payroll.

## The documented pattern, per city

**Sanitation/conservancy is the most contractualised core function in every one of our
cities.** It takes two forms.

*Zone concessions.* **Chennai** is the clearest case: by July 2025, **10 of 15 GCC zones**
were run by private SWM operators — **Urbaser-Sumeet** (7 zones, ~half the city, an
~EUR 480M, 8-year contract from Oct 2020, covering 3.7M people across 92 wards) and **Ramky
Enviro** (4 zones) (Citizen Matters; CenFA). GCC remains the nominal sanitation authority —
the cell stays GREEN — while two-thirds of the city's conservancy is privately operated.

*Contract/casual labour.* This is the more pervasive form. **Hyderabad's** GHMC runs
**~28,000 sanitation workers on contract/outsourcing** — essentially the whole front line —
and pointedly excluded them from a pay revision (The Hans India; Deccan Chronicle). **Mumbai's**
BMC has ~28,000 permanent but ~7,000 contract safai kamgar *plus* ~9,000 "dattak vasti" slum
workers not even counted as contract (Scroll; IndiaSpend). **Kolkata's** KMC keeps its
permanent conservancy cadre half-empty — **~6,000 of ~14,000 sanctioned posts filled** — and
back-fills with casual labour from the 100-days programme at **Rs 202/day** (NewsClick).
**Ahmedabad's** AMC reports ~13,000-14,230 permanent safai kamdar but adds ~5,000 workers via
RWAs / "Sakhi Mandali" / the Valmiki Association, while *denying* it uses "regular contract
workers" — the denial is the mechanism (NewKerala; CLRA). **Bengaluru** is the partial
counter-story: organised pourakarmikas forced regularisation of **~12,650** workers (and
abolished the labour-contractor middleman for them, lifting wages from as little as
Rs 2,000-5,000 to ~Rs 41,000) — yet **~10,000 sweepers and ~9,000 truck crew remain contract**
(The News Minute; The South First).

**Water/sewerage operation, by contrast, is genuinely in-house** in Ahmedabad, Mumbai and
Kolkata — a real positive worth foregrounding. The one full carve-out is **Nagpur**, where
**Orange City Water (Vishvaraj + Veolia)** runs the entire urban water cycle under a 25-year
PPP — a *12th-Schedule* function (water, not electricity) handed to a private operator. It is
the sharpest municipal-core privatisation in the atlas precisely because it is still so rare.

STP O&M and property-tax-discovery (GIS surveys, e.g. GHMC's PTIN drive) are routinely
outsourced too, but per-city contract specifics are largely not public — flagged `verify:true`
rather than guessed.

## The contract safai karmachari: the caste dimension

The contracted-out tier is overwhelmingly Dalit. NAMASTE profiling of ~38,000 sanitation
workers found **68.9% SC, 14.7% OBC, 8.3% ST — ~77% Dalit, ~92% SC/ST/OBC** (The News Minute,
2024); government data puts manual scavengers at **~97% SC**; the 2011 census, ~95%. The
predominant caste is **Valmiki**. CLRA's *Swept Aside* (2025) shows Ahmedabad's Valmikis
funnelled into safai work after the textile mills closed — caste-locked into both permanent
and informal sanitation rolls.

So contractualisation is a **caste-justice machine**. The same sweeping and sewer work is
split into a permanent tier (secure, pensioned, mixed-caste) and a contract tier (precarious,
Dalit, sub-half wages, no tenure, denied safety) — Mumbai pays Rs 24,000 permanent vs
Rs 14,500 contract; Chennai's privatisation *cut* take-home from ~Rs 11,730 to ~Rs 9,000. The
Safai Karmachari Andolan records **300+ sewer/septic-tank deaths a year** (339 in 2022-23),
falling on exactly the contract/informal layer that contracting strips of training, gear and
accountability. This is **Ambedkar's** point in *Annihilation of Caste* made literal: the
caste "division of labour" is also a "**division of labourers**" — graded, hereditary,
unequal. The labour contract is its modern instrument.

## The conceptual payoff

A GREEN/"in-house" cell can be a shell. The elected corporation can hold the seal, the
statute and the org chart for water, waste and sanitation, and still have outsourced the
*operation* — to a Ramky/Urbaser concession, to a Veolia water O&M, or to a Dalit contract
workforce kept off the permanent rolls. Read with Ghosh (the municipality as a corporation
that offloads costly functions while keeping the form) and Cooper (privatisation as a
counterrevolution against democratic claims on public goods), the lesson is that **devolution
must be measured twice**: once for who is *nominally* responsible, and once for who *actually
operates and staffs* the service. Where the two diverge — and in sanitation they almost always
do — the elected body is a contract administrator, and its front line is a caste underclass on
a day-wage.
