# Hyderabad: the city decided elsewhere

Hyderabad is the cleanest specimen in the atlas of a single proposition: **not one
function worth holding was taken from the elected corporation by the elected
corporation.** Every carve-out — water, land, transport, housing, the IT corridor,
the metro — was decided by the *state* (or, before 1948, by the Nizam's princely
government), and one (the Smart City SPV) by the *centre*. On the analytical axis
that matters, **decided_by**, the elected city scores zero. The "unelected city" is,
precisely, the city decided somewhere other than the ballot its residents cast.

## The frame the state legislated for itself

Two facts make Hyderabad distinctive, and both are acts of the state asserting
control *over* the elected body rather than *through* it.

First, the **statutory exemption**. The Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation is
governed by the **Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation Act, 1955 (Act II of
1956)** — and it is **explicitly exempted from the Telangana Municipalities Act,
2019 (Act 11 of 2019, §1(3))**, the general Part-IXA conformity law the state wrote
for every *other* urban local body in Telangana. The megacity corporation is held
outside the standard municipal frame, under a bespoke statute the state keeps under
its own discretionary control. This is depoliticisation by statute: the most
politically valuable urban territory in the state sits under a special regime, with
levers — mayoral reservation, staff transfer, supersession — the state can pull.
Greater Hyderabad itself was *assembled* by the state in 2007, merging the erstwhile
MCH with twelve surrounding bodies — the merged councils did not vote themselves out
of existence.

Second, the **re-municipalisation of the metro**. Hyderabad Metro Rail Phase I (69
km, ~5 lakh riders/day) was built as the world's largest metro PPP — an L&T DBFOT
concession under the state SPV HMRL, commercial operations from November 2017. In
**April 2026 the Government of Telangana acquired Phase I from L&T (~Rs 1,461 crore,
share transfer completed)** — a genuinely rare reversal of a privatised major urban
asset. But read it carefully: the asset moved **private → state**, not to the elected city.
It is re-*state*-isation, not democratisation; `decided_by` stays "state."
(Service-provider records should move from `private` to `spv`/`state`.)

## The carve-outs, pre- and post-1991

The 74th Amendment (passed 1992, in force 1 June 1993) is the hinge. Hyderabad's most
valuable functions were carved out **before** that promise even existed:

- **Water and sewerage → HMWSSB (Act 15 of 1989).** Twelfth-Schedule entries 5 and 6
  — the rent-rich, capital-intensive utility — written into an appointed statutory
  board *the year before* the Amendment promised devolution. An Andhra Pradesh Act,
  adapted to Telangana after the 2014 Reorganisation (Central Act 6 of 2014) via the
  Telangana Adaptation of Laws Order, 2016. This is INDEX.md's central proof: the
  carve-out is affirmatively legislated, not a gap.
- **Metropolitan planning and land → City Improvement Board (Nizam-era, ~1912) →
  HUDA (1975) → HMDA (Act 8 of 2008).** The improvement-trust lineage Anwesha Ghosh
  reconstructs, in its local princely form: planning and land lifted into appointed
  technocrats, "improvement" as the idiom of land control and recoupment. HMDA's
  ~7,257 sq km land machine — land-pooling, development schemes — is the rentier
  function pulled out of the elected body. (The princely board and HUDA are pre-1991;
  HMDA's 2008 re-enactment is post-1991, the same logic re-legislated.)
- **City transport → TSRTC/TGSRTC**, a state corporation under the central Road
  Transport Corporations Act, 1950. The elected GHMC has no transport undertaking
  (contrast Mumbai's BEST). The bus the resident rides is a state monopoly.
- **Public housing → the AP/Telangana Housing apparatus** (now Telangana State
  Housing Corporation; the Double-Bedroom programme is a *state* scheme). The
  weaker-sections/housing functions (#9–11) are delivered by the state and credited
  to the ruling state party, not the elected councillor.

After 1991, the same machine kept carving — now *against the grain* of the
Amendment's promise:

- **Slum improvement & urban-poverty alleviation (#10–11) → MEPMA** and the
  centrally-sponsored scheme apparatus (NULM, PMAY-Urban). Neoliberal conditionality
  (Cooper): central funds arrive with rules and a *state* mission vehicle that routes
  a Twelfth-Schedule entitlement around the elected ward.
- **The IT corridor → HITEC City / Cyberabad (1998+).** Elite capture and land
  financialisation in their sharpest urban form: the most valuable land assembled by
  the state (APIIC/TSIIC) for capital, governed as an investor-facing enclave outside
  GHMC — a premium-governance "technical ghetto."
- **The Smart City SPV (2015+)** — a centre-mandated company that ring-fences chosen
  projects from the elected council as a funding condition. The improvement trust's
  21st-century form: appointed, executive, not deliberative — and here `decided_by`
  is the **centre**.
- **Electricity → TGSPDCL** (context only; not a 12th-Schedule function). Worth one
  note: Hyderabad *keeps power public*, unlike privatised Torrent (Ahmedabad) or CESC
  (Kolkata).

## The hollow centre: sanitation

The one rent-poor, labour-intensive function GHMC unambiguously keeps — sanitation —
is itself a shell. Roughly the **entire ~28,000-strong conservancy front line is on
contract/outsourcing**, not permanent municipal rolls, and a GHMC pay revision was
applied while pointedly **excluding contract staff**. This is Ambedkar's terrain:
the same sweeping and drain-clearing split into a thin permanent tier and a vast
contract tier that is overwhelmingly Dalit/Valmiki (NAMASTE profiling: ~68.9% SC,
~77% Dalit). The contract is the modern instrument of the graded "division of
labourers" — and the most fully contractualised conservancy workforce among the
atlas cities. The elected city is left holding the broom; even the broom is held by
someone the city does not employ.

## The tally

**Eleven carve-outs** (plus the GHMC status-note and the contractualisation layer).
**Pre-1991:** the improvement board, HUDA, HMWSSB, the RTC lineage, the Housing
Board origin. **Post-1991:** HMDA, MEPMA, the metro, HITEC City/Cyberabad, the Smart
City SPV, electricity (context), contractualisation. **decided_by:** state 11,
centre 1, colonial/princely 1, **elected city 0.** Water and land were gone before
the promise; everything since has been carved against it — and when the state took
the metro *back* in 2026, it took it back for itself, not for the people who elect
the corporation.
