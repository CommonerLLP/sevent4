# Kolkata: the governance-fragmentation dossier

*Who decided? Every body that took a function from the elected Kolkata Municipal Corporation — and the colonial, state or central authority that decided it should.*

Kolkata is Anwesha Ghosh's own city, and the keystone of this atlas. Here the carve-out has more than a colonial *resemblance* — it has a colonial *birth-certificate*, and in 2017 it acquired a repealing Act. The pattern is the one Ghosh reconstructs from the 1911 archive: lift the rent-rich function — land, planning, water-works, transit, township real estate — out of the elected council into an unelected, state-appointed, technocratic body, and leave the elected corporation the labour-intensive, low-rent broom. The Kolkata Municipal Corporation (KMC, elected, under WB Act 59 of 1980) keeps water and sewerage genuinely in-house — a real positive, shared only with Mumbai and Ahmedabad. What it does not keep is the city's land, its metropolitan plan, its townships, its metro, and increasingly the security of the workers who hold the broom.

## The centrepiece: the Calcutta Improvement Trust becomes KMDA

In 1911 the Bengal Legislative Council, in consultation with the European Bengal Chamber of Commerce, passed the **Calcutta Improvement Act (Bengal Act V of 1911)** and created the **Calcutta Improvement Trust (CIT)**. Its stated purpose was to rescue "the work of improvement from the stranglehold of representative politics" — to depoliticise development by handing planning, land acquisition and slum clearance to appointed technocrats. The Chamber's own words give the game away: "the Trust is to be essentially an **executive body and not a deliberative organisation**." Only 4 of its 11 trustees came from the elected municipality. This is, almost verbatim, the design rationale that would later justify every water board and development authority in India: efficiency over deliberation, experts over councillors.

Ghosh's sharpest contribution is to read the CIT not as failed planning but as **financialisation**. The Trust was "pre-emptively founded so as to avail the benefits of the recoupment scheme" — it funded itself by selling surplus acquired-and-redeveloped land at *future*-speculative value, "rendering the future available in the present." Of roughly Rs 822 lakh projected expenditure, the centre gave only Rs 50 lakh, Rs 436 lakh came as loan, and **Rs 336 lakh was to be "recovered by recoupment."** Roads were routed through the cheapest (poorest, bustee) land to minimise compensation; the Trust demolished more houses than it built; rehousing the poor "became an idle afterthought." Datta's line is the atlas's thesis written in 1911: "the imperial authorities had been keen to **foist the costs of government to the local populace in the name of devolution**."

What Ghosh reconstructs as a *conceptual* lineage — improvement trust to development authority — is in West Bengal a **literal statutory succession**. Effective **28 April 2017**, the Kolkata Improvement Trust (the renamed CIT) and the Kolkata Metropolitan Water & Sanitation Authority were repealed and "placed under the administrative control and supervision of the **KMDA**" — the Kolkata Metropolitan Development Authority, the state-appointed body whose statutory home is the **WB Town & Country (Planning & Development) Act, 1979 (WB Act XIII of 1979)**. The 2017 repealing/amending Act is cited as WB Acts XIV and XII of 2017 (a citation from secondary sources — UDMA's official history and press — and flagged `verify:true`, since the act numbers are not confirmed against the gazette). The merger is verified; the numbers are not. The colonial improvement trust did not merely *inspire* the modern development authority. It *became* it, by statute, in living memory.

## The full roster — on the axis of who decided

- **CIT (1911, colonial).** The keystone. Depoliticisation, recoupment, elite capture, cost-shifting "devolution." Merged into KMDA 2017.
- **KMDA (1970/1979, state; 2017 absorbs CIT + KMWSA).** The improvement trust reborn — metropolitan land/planning/transit for ~14M people decided by an unelected, minister-chaired board, the recoupment machine with a Calcutta birth-certificate. Nodal agency for JNNURM/AMRUT conditionality.
- **KMWSA (1966, state).** Metropolitan water/sanitation to a state board — though, unusually, core-city water stayed with the elected KMC. Folded into KMDA 2017.
- **WBHIDCO / New Town Rajarhat (1999 SPV; NKDA Act 2007, state) — post-1991.** The recoupment machine's contemporary form: vast Rajarhat farmland assembled (amid contested, coercive acquisition), serviced plots monetised, and the whole township governed by a state SPV and a state development authority — a city run by planners *instead of* an elected council. The purest "unelected city."
- **WB Housing Board (1972, state).** Housing — the function the CIT was founded to deliver and abandoned — sits with a state board, skewed toward saleable stock over the slum upgrading the Twelfth Schedule names.
- **Kolkata Metro (1984, centre).** The sharpest "who decided" of all: India's first metro is a zone of **Indian Railways**, run directly by the Union government — not the city, not even a state SPV. The citizen who elects the KMC councillor has *zero* electoral lever over the transit spine.
- **WBTC + private operators (state).** Bus transport never sat inside the elected corporation (contrast Mumbai's BEST); state corporation plus a privatised, precarious-labour permit fleet.
- **CESC (1899, colonial → private).** The oldest carve-out: a 125-year private electricity monopoly answerable only to the regulator and RPSG shareholders. Outside the Twelfth Schedule, but the template for the unelected city — Cooper's basic public good placed permanently beyond democratic claim.

**Count: 8 bodies. Pre-1991: 5 created pre-1991 (CIT 1911, KMWSA 1966, KMDA origin 1970/79, WBHB 1972, Metro 1984, CESC 1899) — note CESC, CIT, KMWSA and KMDA all *originate* pre-1991. Post-1991: WBHIDCO/New Town (1999/2007) is the one wholly post-1991 body; the decisive 2017 CIT→KMDA merger and WBTC (2016) are post-1991 restructurings of pre-1991 lineages.** So the era split is roughly **6 pre-1991 origins : 1 wholly post-1991 SPV : 2 post-1991 keystone restructurings.**

## The hollowing from within: casualisation

The functions KMC genuinely keeps are hollowed by attrition. Of ~14,000 sanctioned conservancy posts, only ~6,000 are filled; the ~8,000-post gap is plugged by casual workers drawn from the 100-days urban work programme at **~Rs 202/day**, doing hazardous head-load haulage. Leaving the permanent cadre half-empty and back-filling with day-wage casuals is contractualisation by attrition — and the casual tier is overwhelmingly Dalit/Valmiki (nationally ~77% of sanitation workers are Dalit; ~97% of manual scavengers are SC). Ambedkar's formula holds exactly: the "division of labour" is also a "division of labourers" — a secure, pensioned, mixed-caste permanent tier and a precarious Rs-202/day Dalit tier doing identical work under the same municipal supervisors. The in-house/GREEN sanitation cell on the scorecard is one of the most hollow in the atlas.

## Headline

Kolkata is the atlas's proof-by-statute: in 2017 Ghosh's colonial Calcutta Improvement Trust (1911) was literally repealed and merged into the modern development authority KMDA — the depoliticisation-and-recoupment machine completing its century-long arc — while every rent-rich function, from 1911's improvement schemes to 1984's Railway-run metro to 1999's Rajarhat township, was decided by the colonial state, the centre, or the State of West Bengal, and never once by the city Calcuttans actually elect.
