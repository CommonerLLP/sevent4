# Mumbai: the unelected city behind the strongest corporation

Mumbai is the atlas's awkward case. By the scorecard it looks like the *least* hollowed
city in India — the Municipal Corporation of Greater Mumbai (MCGM/BMC), constituted under
the capacious **Mumbai Municipal Corporation Act, 1888** (Bombay Act III of 1888), still
runs water, sewerage, storm-water, roads, street lighting, parks, fire, an unusually deep
estate of municipal hospitals (KEM, Sion, Nair), and — through the BEST Undertaking — the
island city's buses and electricity, all in-house. No other corporation in the set keeps
so much. And yet Mumbai is also where the *rent-rich* functions have been removed from the
elected body most completely. The dossier's point is not that Mumbai's corporation is
weak. It is that **the valuable half of the city was lifted out of the one body Mumbaikars
elect — and almost every removal was decided by the state or the centre, never by the
city.**

## The pre-1991 wave: the developmental state assembles the land

The lineage begins, exactly as Anwesha Ghosh's *improvement regime* predicts, with a
colonial trust. The **Bombay City Improvement Trust (1898)**, created after the 1896
plague, lifted planning, clearance and land development out of the elected corporation into
an appointed executive board — depoliticisation as the founding move, with rehousing the
poor reduced to "an idle afterthought" while the Trust financed itself by selling
redeveloped land. Every later body is a variation on it.

In the developmental decades the *form* was the **state authority**. **CIDCO (1970)**, a
government company designated a New Town Development Authority under the MRTP Act 1966,
built Navi Mumbai across the harbour by acquiring village land at agricultural prices and
monetising it as a planned-city landlord — the single largest public land machine in the
region, decided wholly by the state, with the displaced Agri-Koli villagers left on a
partly-unkept "12.5% scheme." **MMRDA (Maharashtra Act IV of 1975)** took metropolitan
planning and the megaproject pipeline; it self-finances off land banks like the
Bandra-Kurla Complex, making it a metropolitan rentier in Ghosh's recoupment line, and it
quietly displaces the constitutionally-mandated, part-elected Metropolitan Planning
Committee (Art. 243ZE). **MHADA (1976)** consolidated five housing boards into one
state-appointed custodian of the city's most valuable redevelopment asset — old cessed
buildings on prime island land — turning a welfare-housing mandate into a TDR-and-FSI
lever. Three authorities, three slices of land and planning, **decided by the state in
every case.**

## The post-1991 wave: slums become real estate, and the SPV arrives

After liberalisation the instrument changed from the authority to the **special-purpose
vehicle and the PPP** — off the municipal books, single-purpose, increasingly
private-majority. The hinge is the **Slum Rehabilitation Authority (1995)**, constituted by
amending the Maharashtra Slum Areas Act 1971 and notified as the planning authority for
Mumbai's slums. It is the purest land-financialisation instrument in the atlas: ~40% of
Mumbaikars become a planning input, developers cross-subsidise free rehab flats with
free-sale FSI and TDR, and the rent flows out while the scheme routinely under-houses the
Dalit-Muslim poor it claims to serve. The elected corporation is bypassed; the appointed
board is a site of open builder capture.

Then the SPVs: **Mumbai Smart City (2015)**, a centrally-mandated corporate vehicle with an
appointed board controlling Mission funds inside the city's own area; **MMRC (2014)** and
**Maha Metro (2016)**, 50:50 GoI-GoM joint ventures building the metro and holding its
station/depot real estate — Cooper's counterrevolution in institutional form, ring-fencing
megaproject debt and revenue from democratic budgeting. Meanwhile **BEST**, the BMC's own
in-house transit, is starved (its ridership collapsing) as capital flows to the off-the-
books metro. **Austerity for the bus the commuter rides; extravagance for the metro the
state builds.** The line terminates at the **Dharavi Redevelopment SPV (Navbharat Mega
Developers, ~2023)**, in which a single private conglomerate holds the controlling stake
and the Government of Maharashtra a minority — elite capture written straight into the cap
table, over ~240 hectares of the most valuable slum land in India. Alongside, the suburban
**electricity grid (Adani, 2018; Tata)** and **both airports (Adani, 2021/2025)** put tens
of millions of daily users under monopolies they cannot vote out — democratic deficit,
even where the function was never strictly municipal.

## The hollowing inside the in-house cell

Even the functions BMC keeps are hollowed from within. Sanitation is "in-house" on the org
chart, but ~7,000 counted contract workers plus ~9,000 uncounted *dattak vasti* workers do
identical conservancy work beside ~28,000 permanent staff, at roughly half the wage and
none of the tenure. This front line is overwhelmingly Dalit/Valmiki (~77% Dalit in NAMASTE
profiling), and regularisation has come only by Supreme Court order, never as policy —
Ambedkar's "division of labour is also a division of labourers," reproduced as a contract.
This is the one hollowing the city itself decided.

## Who decided

That is the dossier's spine. Of roughly twelve carve-outs, the elected city decided **none**
of the major land, slum, housing, transit or port transfers. The only "city"-decided items
are BEST staying in (and being starved) and the corporation contractualising its own
sweepers. Mumbai keeps the broadest municipal service estate in India — and still its land,
its slums, its housing, its metro, its port and its suburban grid were lifted out of the
one body its residents elect, by governments they did not.
