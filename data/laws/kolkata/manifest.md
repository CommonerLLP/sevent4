## CIT / Calcutta Improvement Act 1911 — current status (key historical point)

The Calcutta Improvement Act, 1911 (Bengal Act V of 1911) is **no longer the operative, free-standing framework it once was.** Per the West Bengal Dept. of Urban Development & Municipal Affairs (UDMA) official history page:

- CIT began operating in **1912** under the 1911 Act, developing urban infrastructure for Calcutta.
- In **2017** the State merged the Kolkata Improvement Trust (KIT, the renamed CIT) and the Kolkata Metropolitan Water & Sanitation Authority (KMWSA) **into the KMDA**.
- KIT and KMWSA were "placed under the administrative control and supervision of the KMDA **w.e.f. 28.04.2017**" in terms of the *Kolkata Improvement Trust and Kolkata Metropolitan Water & Sanitation Authority repealing Act and amendment of the West Bengal Town & Country (Planning & Development) Act, 2017.*
- Effect: the **Calcutta Improvement Act, 1911 was repealed** and the CIT/KIT ceased to exist as an independent statutory body; its functions now sit with KMDA under the 1979 Act framework.

So for the atlas: **CIT is defunct as an independent body (merged into KMDA, 28 April 2017); the 1911 Act is repealed.** Treat KMDA (under the 1979 Act) as the successor for improvement/development functions in the Kolkata Metropolitan Area.

## Which body holds which 12th-Schedule function (Art. 243W)

The Twelfth Schedule functions in the Kolkata Metropolitan Area are split between the **KMC** (the elected urban local body for the core city) and **KMDA** (the State-controlled metropolitan development authority):

- **KMC (under the 1980 Act, esp. s.29 obligatory functions):** potable water supply, drainage & sewerage, solid-waste/filth removal, public streets, public health (vaccination, infectious-disease control), street lighting, regulation of land use & building within the corporation area, public conveniences — i.e., the day-to-day "urban local self-government" basket for the city proper.
- **KMDA (under the 1979 Act):** metropolitan-scale urban *planning and development* — water supply, sewerage & drainage, traffic & transportation, township/area development, housing & slum improvement, parks/playgrounds across the broader Kolkata Metropolitan Area; and it is the Technical Secretariat to the Kolkata Metropolitan Planning Committee (KMPC, the Art. 243ZE district/metropolitan planning committee).

Caveat: this split is functional/administrative, drawn from the Acts and the KMDA/UDMA descriptions, not a clause-by-clause statutory allocation. Note the overlap (both touch water/drainage/transport) — KMDA operates at metropolitan/capital-works scale, KMC at municipal-service scale. Article 243W lets the State devolve any Twelfth-Schedule matter; West Bengal has retained much development/planning capacity in the State-controlled KMDA rather than fully devolving to KMC.
