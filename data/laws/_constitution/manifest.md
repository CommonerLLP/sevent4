# Constitutional / Common Legal Layer — Municipal Governance

The foundational legal frame for the civic-accountability atlas. Every Indian
city's local government derives its constitutional existence, mandate, and
finances from this layer: **Part IXA of the Constitution of India**, inserted by
**The Constitution (Seventy-fourth Amendment) Act, 1992**, together with the
**Twelfth Schedule**.

This manifest records exact citations, verbatim primary text for the
load-bearing provisions, and the downloaded source files. No text below is
paraphrased unless explicitly labelled "Note" — verbatim quotations are taken
from the official Ministry of Law and Justice text of the Act and the India Code
edition of the Constitution.

## 2. Part IXA — THE MUNICIPALITIES (Articles 243P to 243ZG)

Full verbatim text of all Part IXA articles is in **`part-IXA-articles.txt`**
(extracted from the official Act PDF). The articles, in order:

- **243P** — Definitions
- **243Q** — Constitution of Municipalities (Nagar Panchayat / Municipal Council / Municipal Corporation)
- **243R** — Composition of Municipalities
- **243S** — Constitution and composition of Wards Committees, etc.
- **243T** — Reservation of seats (SC/ST, women ≥ one-third, backward classes at State option)
- **243U** — Duration of Municipalities (fixed 5-year term; elections within 6 months of dissolution)
- **243V** — Disqualifications for membership
- **243W** — Powers, authority and responsibilities of Municipalities (see verbatim below)
- **243X** — Power to impose taxes by, and Funds of, the Municipalities
- **243Y** — Finance Commission (see verbatim below)
- **243Z** — Audit of accounts of Municipalities
- **243ZA** — Elections to the Municipalities (State Election Commission)
- **243ZB** — Application to Union territories
- **243ZC** — Part not to apply to certain areas (Scheduled/tribal areas, Darjeeling GTC)
- **243ZD** — Committee for district planning (District Planning Committee)
- **243ZE** — Committee for Metropolitan planning (Metropolitan Planning Committee)
- **243ZF** — Continuance of existing laws and Municipalities
- **243ZG** — Bar to interference by courts in electoral matters

## 3. Article 243W — Powers, authority and responsibilities of Municipalities (VERBATIM)

> **243W. Powers, authority and responsibilities of Municipalities, etc.—**
> Subject to the provisions of this Constitution, the Legislature of a State
> **may, by law, endow—**
>
> (a) the Municipalities with such powers and authority as may be necessary to
> enable them to function as institutions of self-government and such law may
> contain provisions for the devolution of powers and responsibilities upon
> Municipalities, subject to such conditions as may be specified therein, with
> respect to—
>
> &nbsp;&nbsp;(i) the preparation of plans for economic development and social justice;
>
> &nbsp;&nbsp;(ii) the performance of functions and the implementation of schemes as may be
> entrusted to them including those in relation to the matters listed in the
> Twelfth Schedule;
>
> (b) the Committees with such powers and authority as may be necessary to enable
> them to carry out the responsibilities conferred upon them including those in
> relation to the matters listed in the Twelfth Schedule.

Source: `74th-amendment-1992.pdf` (Ministry of Law text), Part IXA, Art. 243W.

## 4. Article 243Y — Finance Commission (VERBATIM)

> **243Y. Finance Commission.—**(1) The Finance Commission constituted under
> article 243-I shall also review the financial position of the Municipalities
> and make recommendations to the Governor as to—
>
> (a) the principles which should govern—
>
> &nbsp;&nbsp;(i) the distribution between the State and the Municipalities of the net
> proceeds of the taxes, duties, tolls and fees leviable by the State, which may
> be divided between them under this Part and the allocation between the
> Municipalities at all levels of their respective shares of such proceeds;
>
> &nbsp;&nbsp;(ii) the determination of the taxes, duties, tolls and fees which may be
> assigned to, or appropriated by, the Municipalities;
>
> &nbsp;&nbsp;(iii) the grants-in-aid to the Municipalities from the Consolidated Fund of the
> State;
>
> (b) the measures needed to improve the financial position of the Municipalities;
>
> (c) any other matter referred to the Finance Commission by the Governor in the
> interests of sound finance of the Municipalities.
>
> (2) The Governor shall cause every recommendation made by the Commission under
> this article together with an explanatory memorandum as to the action taken
> thereon to be laid before the Legislature of the State.

Source: `74th-amendment-1992.pdf`, Part IXA, Art. 243Y.
(Note: Art. 243-I, referenced here, is in Part IX — the State Finance
Commission constituted for Panchayats, whose remit Art. 243Y extends to
Municipalities.)

## 5. The Twelfth Schedule — the 18 functions (FULL VERBATIM LIST)

Heading: **"TWELFTH SCHEDULE (Article 243W)"**. Verbatim list (also in
`twelfth-schedule.txt`):

1. Urban planning including town planning.
2. Regulation of land-use and construction of buildings.
3. Planning for economic and social development.
4. Roads and bridges.
5. Water supply for domestic, industrial and commercial purposes.
6. Public health, sanitation conservancy and solid waste management.
7. Fire services.
8. Urban forestry, protection of the environment and promotion of ecological aspects.
9. Safeguarding the interests of weaker sections of society, including the handicapped and mentally retarded.
10. Slum improvement and upgradation.
11. Urban poverty alleviation.
12. Provision of urban amenities and facilities such as parks, gardens, playgrounds.
13. Promotion of cultural, educational and aesthetic aspects.
14. Burials and burial grounds; cremations, cremation grounds and electric crematoriums.
15. Cattle pounds; prevention of cruelty to animals.
16. Vital statistics including registration of births and deaths.
17. Public amenities including street lighting, parking lots, bus stops and public conveniences.
18. Regulation of slaughter houses and tanneries.

Source: `74th-amendment-1992.pdf`, Sec. 4 ("Addition of Twelfth Schedule"); also
present in `constitution-of-india-full-indiacode.pdf`.

## 6. Other targeted provisions (summaries; full text in `part-IXA-articles.txt`)

- **Article 243ZD — Committee for district planning.** Every State shall
  constitute a **District Planning Committee** at the district level to
  consolidate the plans of the Panchayats and Municipalities in the district and
  prepare a draft development plan for the district as a whole. ≥ four-fifths of
  members to be elected by/from the elected members of the district-level
  Panchayat and the district's Municipalities, in proportion to rural:urban
  population.

- **Article 243ZE — Committee for Metropolitan planning.** Every **Metropolitan
  area** shall constitute a **Metropolitan Planning Committee** to prepare a
  draft development plan for the area as a whole. ≥ two-thirds of members to be
  elected by/from the elected members of the area's Municipalities and Panchayat
  Chairpersons, in proportion to Municipality:Panchayat population.

- **Article 243ZF — Continuance of existing laws and Municipalities.**
  Notwithstanding Part IXA, any pre-commencement State municipal law
  inconsistent with Part IXA continues only until amended/repealed by a competent
  Legislature **or until the expiration of one year from such commencement
  (i.e., effectively up to ~1 June 1994), whichever is earlier.** This is the
  clause that forced every State to enact **conformity legislation** within one
  year. Existing Municipalities continued until the end of their term unless
  sooner dissolved.

## 7. Legal note — why devolution is OPTIONAL / State-discretionary

This is the legal root of the atlas's central argument about uneven municipal
capacity across cities.

- **The conformity-legislation requirement.** Part IXA is self-executing only as
  to structure (existence of Municipalities, 5-year terms, reservations, State
  Election Commission, State Finance Commission). Article 243ZF gave States **one
  year** from commencement to bring their municipal statutes into conformity.
  Every State therefore had to pass (or amend) its own Municipal Act / Municipal
  Corporations Act. The result is **18 functions defined uniformly by the
  Constitution, but delivered through 28+ different State legal regimes.**

- **"may, by law, endow" — the discretionary trigger.** The pivotal language is
  in **Article 243W**: the State Legislature *"**may**, by law, **endow**"*
  Municipalities with powers and authority, including responsibility for "the
  matters listed in the Twelfth Schedule." The verb is **"may," not "shall."**
  Likewise Article 243X uses *"may, by law"* for taxation powers. By contrast,
  the structural articles (243Q duration, 243T reservations, 243Y/243I Finance
  Commission) use **"shall."**

- **Consequence.** The Twelfth Schedule is **not a mandatory devolution list** —
  it is a *menu of permissible subjects.* A State may devolve all 18 functions,
  some, or (formally) none of them to its Municipalities; it may also keep
  functions, funds, or functionaries with State-level parastatals (water boards,
  development authorities, slum boards) rather than the elected Municipality.
  Devolution of **functions, finances, and functionaries (the "3 Fs")** is thus a
  matter of **State political will**, not constitutional compulsion. This is
  precisely why a city's *de jure* municipal mandate (the 18 functions) routinely
  diverges from its *de facto* control over services and budgets — the gap the
  atlas measures city by city.
